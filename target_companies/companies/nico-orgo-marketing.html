Part 1

A certified niche capability, in genuine and severe decline

This is the steepest decline found across every company profiled in this project so far: revenue fell from ~₹177 Cr (FY22) to ₹70.53 Cr (FY24), a roughly 60% drop over two years, with EBITDA down 89.27% year-on-year and both net margin (-2.64%) and ROCE (-31.96%) deeply negative in the most recent period.

What survives the decline: a WHO-GMP approved, ISO-6-grade clean-room facility and REACH registration — real, checkable certifications for pharmaceutical-grade nicotine derivatives (USP/EP grade nicotine, nicotine polacrilex, nicotine bitartrate) — plus the HVT sheet's own claim that it is India's leading USP/EP-grade nicotine supplier.

It exports to the USA, Colombia, Australia, South Africa and Bangladesh — a genuinely international customer base for a company this size, per the HVT sheet's own gap research, though the specific % of revenue from exports wasn't found in this pass.

A separate, unresolved discrepancy: one company-associated source describes the underlying business as founded in 1980 with '40+ years of experience,' likely referring to a predecessor or affiliated entity rather than the legal entity behind this CIN (incorporated 1998) — the HVT sheet doesn't flag this specifically, but it's worth noting alongside the financial picture.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field's own size estimates disagree by nearly 30x, a genuine methodology problem worth flagging on its own.

    Nicotine replacement therapy market estimates range from just $6.9B by 2033 to $198.71B by 2030, depending on whether 'NRT market' is defined narrowly (patches/gums/lozenges) or broadly (including e-cigarettes/heat-not-burn) — treat any single figure with real caution.

  • Regulatory purity and certification are the entry gate, not brand or price.

    Pharmaceutical-grade purity (USP/EP compliance), WHO-GMP and REACH certification are what allow a bulk nicotine-API supplier to sell into regulated NRT/vape formulators at all.

  • This is a genuinely thin-peer niche — no directly comparable listed Indian nicotine-API manufacturer was found.

    The named market participants (Haleon/Nicorette, Pfizer, J&J, GSK) are all brand/formulation owners, not API suppliers like Nico Orgo — there's no clean apples-to-apples benchmark available.

A certified, internationally-exporting niche API supplier whose revenue has collapsed by more than half in two years, in a market whose own size estimates can't agree within an order of magnitude. This is a company to watch closely, not one to assume will recover.

Market data: Grand View Research, Coherent Market Insights, Bonafide Research, GlobeNewswire — figures diverge sharply by methodology, ranges shown rather than a single number. No clean peer set exists for this niche.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Margin

A ~60% two-year revenue decline needs a specific cause identified (lost customer, raw-material/tobacco sourcing disruption, regulatory change, demand shift) before any market-expansion opportunity is worth pursuing — the diagnosis, not the opportunity, is the actual next step.
