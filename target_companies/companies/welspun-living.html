Part 1

A deep-tech bet on manufacturing its way around the tariff that just hurt it

FY26 was a genuinely difficult year: revenue fell 11% to ₹9,399 Cr, PAT fell 67% to ₹213 Cr, and OPM compressed from 12% to 8% — the company itself attributes this to tariff-related shipment deferrals and unfavorable product mix, not a demand collapse. ROE held at 11%.

It holds 47+ patented technologies including Nanocore® (allergen barrier), Wel-Trak® (supply-chain traceability) and HYGROCOTTON® (absorbency/softness process), plus OEKO-TEX Standard 100, GOTS, GRS certification and 'Made in Green by OEKO-TEX' status — the first Indian bed/bath manufacturer to receive it. It also ranks in DJSI's top 3 percentile globally on ESG.

It's the #1 home-textiles supplier to the US market, with exports at ~67% US, ~17% EU and distribution in 50+ countries — genuinely concentrated exposure to exactly the market where the tariff pressure originates.

It responded with real capital, not just commentary: a $50M Ohio pillow plant (6.7M-pillow initial capacity, operational since ~September 2024) and a $13M Nevada pillow plant (commercial production began March 2026) — US-based manufacturing that sidesteps the tariff exposure directly. FY25 capex was ₹860 Cr, with ₹577 Cr planned for FY26, plus a ₹304 Cr terry-towel facility expansion and a ₹121 Cr Anjar modernization approved mid-2026.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large, with India's own segment growing at a comparable or faster clip, depending on methodology.

    Global home textiles: $230.99B (2025) to $338.37B by 2032 (5.60%/yr). India home textile market: $4.51B (2025) to $6.78B by 2034 (4.65%/yr) per one source, or as fast as 8.4%/yr per another.

  • Every major listed peer shows the same recent pain — this is a sector-wide tariff shock, not a Welspun-specific stumble.

    Indo Count Industries' FY26 PAT fell 49.33% YoY on nearly flat revenue, with ROE dropping from a 16.93% 5-year average to 9.92% in the latest half-year — the same margin-compression pattern visible at Welspun.

  • Trident Ltd shows the segment can still deliver strong results, a useful counterpoint.

    Trident posted ₹6,933 Cr TTM revenue with ₹409 Cr TTM profit and 11.34% ROE, with Q1 FY27 profit up 55% QoQ — proof the tariff shock hasn't uniformly depressed every player equally.

The largest, most patent-rich player in Indian home textiles just had its worst profit year in the recent record, for a reason (US tariffs) that's hitting the whole sector — and it's the one responding with real US-based manufacturing capacity, not just cost-cutting.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Velocity

Building domestic B2C presence toward 50,000 stores by FY27

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: Welspun dominates as India's largest home-textile exporter and #1 US supplier, but its domestic Indian market share remains a comparatively untapped opportunity — the company has stated a specific target of strengthening B2C presence to reach 50,000 stores by FY27.

HVT gap research (welspunliving.com).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Welspun Living's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.
