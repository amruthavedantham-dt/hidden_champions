Part 1

A genuinely certified capability, mostly serving its own group

Revenue reached ₹151 Cr in FY25, up 8.96% year-on-year — steady, if not fast, growth — with no PAT, EBITDA margin or ROE figure found for the standalone India entity.

Its AS9100D and Airbus Approved Supplier certifications are third-party sourced to an actual TUV SUD certificate document, not just a company claim — the strongest-credibility moat evidence available for this company, even though the HVT sheet still marks it `needs_verify: yes`.

A structural nuance worth naming plainly: the HVT sheet's own gap research, sourced to trade data, states that Recaero India's major buyers are Recaero France (its own parent) and Safran Aircraft Engine — meaning a meaningful share of its 'export' activity is intra-group, not open-market sales to external customers. This isn't a red flag (captive manufacturing arms are a normal model in aerospace), but it changes what 'export capability' means here.

It runs 17+ CNC machines (up to 9-axis, 3-axis turn-mill) machining aluminum, Inconel, titanium and 15-5PH alloys, plus a dedicated sheet-metal shop and OEM-qualified welding (TIG, Plasma, Spot) — real, specific manufacturing capability across the hardest aerospace alloys.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and structurally favored by India's own policy direction.

    India aerospace parts manufacturing: $13.6B (2023) to $21.48B by 2030 (6.8%/yr); India's defense indigenization is expected to rise from under 40% to potentially 70%+ over the coming decade.

  • AS9100/NADCAP certification is the real gate, and Recaero India already clears it — the harder question is whether that clearance is being used beyond the group.

    AS9100 certification functions as a de facto requirement for OEM approved-vendor-list status; Recaero India holds it independently of its parent relationship, meaning the qualification itself could support external customers too.

  • The closest listed peer shows what a genuinely externally-diversified version of this business looks like.

    Azad Engineering posts ₹590 Cr revenue (+30% YoY) with 36.9% EBITDA margin, supplying GE, Honeywell, Rolls-Royce, Safran and Siemens as named external customers, plus new contracts with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries and Baker Hughes — a materially more diversified customer base than Recaero India's current parent-plus-Safran concentration.

A genuinely certified, well-equipped precision-machining operation whose customer base is still mostly its own French parent group. The certification is real and portable — the question is whether the company will use it to diversify beyond the family.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Velocity

Expanding export activity to external global buyers beyond Recaero SA

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: current export activity is concentrated on the parent group (Recaero France) and Safran, with clear stated potential to expand to external global buyers beyond the family group — the AS9100D/Airbus-approved certification already exists to support that expansion.

HVT gap research (Volza trade data on Recaero SA).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Recaero India's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Pursue one external OEM RFQ using existing AS9100D/Airbus credentials

Use the already-earned AS9100D and Airbus Approved Supplier status to respond to one external OEM's RFQ (outside the Recaero/Safran relationship) as a test case, measuring how much of the qualification burden the existing certifications actually remove.
