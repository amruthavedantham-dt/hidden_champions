Part 1

A deep-tech bet on the next curve

Mantra Softech doesn't disclose PAT, EBITDA margin or ROE — normal for a private company at this scale — but revenue was ₹429 Cr in FY24-25, and it just closed its first institutional funding round, $14 million, led by India SME Fund II with participation from Motilal Oswal Principal Investments.

The capital is earmarked for R&D, IP-portfolio expansion, and a new Center of Excellence for AI-powered computer vision and security — a platform bet, not a working-capital raise.

The technology is real, not just marketing: a US patent for OCT-based fingerprint liveness detection, devices certified to FBI PIV-071006 and STQC L1 (the gate for Aadhaar authentication hardware), and what the company describes as India's first dedicated biometric "Spoof Lab" for liveness/anti-spoof testing in Ahmedabad.

Hardware is already in production across 50+ countries, having processed over 1.5 billion biometric transactions — a scale claim from the company's own materials, not yet independently corroborated with a transaction-count source.

A licensing deal with Fingerprint Cards AB (Sweden) brings iris-recognition technology in-house, explicitly framed by the company as a step toward new export markets in Africa, the Middle East and Southeast Asia.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is growing faster than most manufacturing categories.

    Global biometric identity verification: $8.88B (2025) to $17.81B by 2030, ~14.9%/yr — among the faster-growing categories in this batch of companies.

  • India's own market is anchored by one thing: Aadhaar.

    India biometrics: $2.8B (2024) to $7.36B by 2033, 11.33%/yr, resting on ~1.2B+ Aadhaar enrollments (~95% of the population) and UIDAI's mandatory 'Registered Device' certification regime.

  • Certification is the real moat in this market, not brand or price.

    UIDAI requires STQC L1/FBI-PIV certified 'Registered Devices' for Aadhaar authentication — an uncertified device simply cannot legally serve this market, regardless of price or performance.

  • The closest domestic peer is growing even faster off a smaller base.

    Precision Biometric India (Chennai) posted ₹65.4 Cr revenue in FY24-25 with 76% 1-year revenue CAGR and 108% EBITDA CAGR — Mantra is roughly 6.5x its revenue scale but the category itself is clearly in a growth phase for multiple players at once.

  • Global majors operate at a different order of magnitude.

    IDEMIA (France) reported ~€2.9B revenue in 2023 before a 2024 restructuring and reported sale process — a scale gap Mantra isn't close to bridging, but also not directly competing against in its current India/Africa/ME/SEA footprint.

A certified, patent-holding device maker in a 15%-a-year market, just given fresh capital to build the AI layer on top of hardware it already ships at scale. The story here is what the funding round says about where the company thinks the next curve is.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Two places worth watching, both already visible in the company's own recent moves.

Margin

Turning certified hardware into a software/AI platform

The fresh funding is explicitly earmarked for an AI/computer-vision Center of Excellence, on top of hardware that already runs 1.5B+ transactions. If that shift lands, the revenue mix moves from one-time hardware sales toward higher-margin software/platform revenue.

Funding use: biometricupdate.com.

Velocity

The Fingerprint Cards licensing deal as an export lever

The Sweden-sourced iris technology was licensed explicitly to expand into new export markets. Africa, the Middle East and Southeast Asia are named directly — this is a stated, not speculative, growth direction.

Finansavisen disclosure.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Mantra's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Margin

Pilot one AI/computer-vision feature as a paid add-on

Before building the full Center of Excellence roadmap, take one AI feature (e.g. advanced liveness scoring) and offer it as a paid add-on to a handful of existing government/enterprise customers, and measure uptake.

Velocity

Track the iris-tech launch market by market

Roll the Fingerprint Cards-licensed iris product out to one new export market at a time (starting with an existing Africa/ME/SEA relationship) and measure adoption before committing wider distribution spend.
