Part 1

Real, fast growth — but the marquee R&D claim doesn't hold up

Revenue reached ₹128 Cr in FY25 with a striking 24% 1-year CAGR, and EBITDA grew 87.79% YoY alongside a 30.1% net worth increase — a genuinely fast-growing transformer manufacturer by the numbers available (PAT, margins, ROE, and ROCE remain paywalled/not found).

It manufactures instrument, control, power, and distribution transformers up to 33kV with custom designs, and holds ISO 9001:2015 certification confirmed independently — a real, verified quality-management credential, alongside CE certification and an ISI license (though the specific 100-3000 Amps amperage range self-claimed in the HVT sheet was not independently corroborated beyond a general ISI reference).

The most consequential finding this session: the HVT sheet's self-claimed '200+ patents and patent-pending technologies' plus DSIR-recognized R&D unit — sourced only from a Scribd marketing brochure — could not be corroborated anywhere. A direct Google Patents assignee search for 'Kalpa Electrikal' returned zero matches; an IP India targeted search returned no results; and the company's own facilities page describes a 'development department' with CAD tooling and testing labs (HV, Partial Discharge, Omicron CT Analyzer) but makes no mention of patents or DSIR recognition at all. This should be treated as unverified, likely marketing exaggeration, not a confirmed credential.

It is actively recruiting export channel-partners across Africa for power transformer sales (a live, dated 2025 push), though no specific country list, signed distributor names, or export revenue percentage were found.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A market where vendor estimates disagree by nearly 2x, but a real, funded government demand driver exists.

    India instrument transformers market: $311.4M (2025) to $461.4M by 2034 (4.47%/yr) per IMARC, vs. $615.4M (2022) to $831.79M by 2028 (5.1%/yr) per Research and Markets. India's RDSS scheme (₹3.03 lakh crore outlay) funds distribution transformer replacement across 52.53 lakh sanctioned units, though rollout is running behind schedule.

  • A real, better-documented Indian peer exists for comparison: Pragati Electricals shows similarly fast growth with cleaner disclosure.

    Pragati Electricals Pvt Ltd posted ₹226 Cr FY25 revenue (+28% 1-yr CAGR) and EBITDA growth of +45.28% YoY, making epoxy resin cast MV/HV instrument transformers — a genuinely comparable-scale peer, though Kalpa's own ROE/ROCE remain unavailable for direct comparison.

  • DISCOM tenders are L1 (lowest-bidder) driven — a structural margin pressure that applies regardless of a supplier's technical credentials.

    The dominant procurement channel in this segment (DISCOM/RDSS-linked tenders) rewards price competitiveness over differentiation, meaning even a well-certified, fast-growing manufacturer like Kalpa faces structural margin ceiling pressure from this channel.

A genuinely fast-growing transformer manufacturer on the topline and EBITDA-growth numbers available — but the specific R&D/patent credential that would most differentiate it from L1-tender-driven competitors doesn't survive independent verification. The growth story stands on its own; the patent claim should be dropped or directly re-verified with the company before being repeated.
