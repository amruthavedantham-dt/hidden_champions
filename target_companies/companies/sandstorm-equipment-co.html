Part 1

A capability with almost no public paper trail

Sandstorm Equipment Co. has essentially no public financial disclosure — revenue, PAT, margins and ROE are all genuinely not found, not merely hard to find, and that is expected for a sole proprietorship with no MCA/ROC filing obligation rather than a sign of distress.

The HVT sheet records a claim of being a trusted supplier of surface-finishing and coating equipment to the Indian Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Air Force) — self-claimed, sourced to a directory listing, and not independently corroborated in this research pass.

Its own export claim doesn't fully hold up against trade-shipment data: the company states 20-40% export share across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, UK and Germany, but Volza's trade-shipment records show only 8 recorded shipments to 2 buyers historically, almost entirely to the UAE.

No patents, certifications (including any AS9100-style qualification a defense/aerospace-adjacent supplier would typically carry) or R&D headcount were found — the product range (blast cabinets, peening machines, paint booths, dust collectors) is real and documented, but the technical-differentiation evidence beyond the company's own claims is thin.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • Estimates for the field diverge sharply by source.

    Shot blasting machines alone: ~$1.3-1.5B (2024-26) to ~$2.05-2.68B by 2032-35, 3.65-7.70%/yr depending on methodology. Shot peening + blasting combined: ~$1.2B (2023) to ~$2.1B by 2032, 6.3%/yr.

  • The field is led by a global major several hundred times Sandstorm's scale.

    Wheelabrator Group (part of Norican Group), the acknowledged global leader, reports ~$776.5M in revenue across 1,000-5,000 employees and 35 locations.

  • No clean public peer set exists for a small Indian player in this exact niche.

    Named comparable Indian manufacturers (Aerowheel Surface Finishing, Surface International) have no disclosed revenue or margin figures; other global names (Sinto America, Empire Airblast, Gostol TST, Rösler) have no segment-specific financials either.

  • The structural demand drivers are real but not Sandstorm-specific.

    Aerospace fatigue-resistance specs and automotive lightweighting both drive precision shot-peening/blasting demand generally; Industry 4.0 (sensors, monitoring, predictive maintenance) is being integrated into blasting lines industry-wide.

A 30-year-old specialist in a market growing mid-single-digits, with a defense-supplier claim and an export claim that both outrun what's independently checkable. The gap between what's said and what's on record is itself the thing worth noting here.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One place worth watching, built on the company's own stated growth market.

Velocity

The global airless blast equipment market

The HVT row's own gap research cites a growing global airless blast equipment market (~$850M by 2030, per industry press). Sandstorm's actual recorded exports (8 shipments, 2 buyers) suggest this growth hasn't yet translated into meaningful order volume abroad.

Market figure: openpr.com (via HVT row gap_source). Export reality: Volza trade data.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Sandstorm's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Convert one existing UAE relationship into a repeat account

Trade data shows real, if thin, UAE shipment history. Before chasing new geographies, test whether the existing UAE buyer relationship can be turned into a recurring order rather than a one-off.
