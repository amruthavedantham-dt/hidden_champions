Part 1

A niche capability, on capital-equipment-style lumpy revenue

The financial picture is genuinely volatile in a way consistent with project-based capital-equipment revenue: the most recent reported year shows 22.16% net sales growth, but an earlier year showed revenue up 285.13% while profit fell 27.43% in the same period — large swings typical of lumpy order recognition, not a steady operating business. No PAT, EBITDA margin or ROE figure was found for any year.

It was founded by G.N. Sharma, a mechanical engineer with a background at Mecon, SAIL and other PSU steel plants — a founding story that maps directly onto its 40+ year specialization in hot and cold rolling mills and process lines for flat products.

It holds Star Export House Certification and has manufactured and installed cold rolling mills in China, Bangladesh and the USA, with orders currently being executed for Japan, Tanzania and Dubai — a genuinely global order book for a company this size.

It runs an R&D centre and offers advanced products including 6-Hi reversing cold mills and HR slitting lines — self-claimed, and the HVT sheet marks `product_improvement` `needs_verify: no` even though the source is a general engineering directory rather than an independent registry.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is mid-sized and China-dominated, with India a distant second.

    China holds ~50% of the global cold rolling mill equipment market, India ~20%; global estimates range $6.35-8.82B by 2030 for the machinery-specific definition (4.8%/yr).

  • India's own steel-capacity build-out is a direct, large-scale structural driver.

    India's crude steelmaking capacity is at ~222 MTPA (June 2026), targeting 300 MTPA by 2030, requiring ~65 MT of additional capacity and ~₹10 lakh crore of envisioned investment — exactly the kind of capex cycle that drives rolling-mill-equipment orders.

  • Global leaders operate at a completely different scale, and no comparable Indian peer with public financials was found.

    SMS Group, Danieli and Primetals Technologies are the dominant global players, investing heavily in Industry 4.0 and advanced-alloy rolling capability; named smaller Indian peers (Steefo Industries, Rana Udyog, Evo Tech, Bhilai Engineering) have no public revenue/margin/ROE figures.

A genuinely global export order book — Japan, Tanzania, Dubai orders in progress alongside historical China/Bangladesh/USA installations — sitting on top of financial data too lumpy to draw a clean trend from. This is a capability story more than a numbers story right now.
