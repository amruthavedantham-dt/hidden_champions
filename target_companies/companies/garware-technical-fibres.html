Part 1

A capability the margin hasn't caught up to

Revenue was roughly flat, ₹1,540 Cr to ₹1,529 Cr (down ~0.7%), while PAT fell about 14%, ₹232 Cr to ₹199 Cr, as EBITDA margin compressed from ~21% to ~19% — Q2 FY26 operating margin touched an 8-quarter low of 13.92% before partly recovering.

Return on equity still held at 18.07% in Q4 FY26 — the balance sheet stayed healthy even as the P&L softened.

The company holds a dominant niche position most financials don't show: roughly 90% of Canada's, 80% of Scotland's, and 30-35% each of Chile's and Norway's aquaculture cage-net supply, on patented anti-fouling ('V2') net technology from a DSIR-registered R&D unit with a reported ~90-101 patents filed.

Recognized a Four Star Export House by India's DGFT; ISO 9001:2015 and ISO 14001:2015 certified; sells in 75+ countries through six overseas offices, with export share rising from ~57% (FY24) to ~61% (H1 FY25).

In July 2025 it acquired two Norway-based firms — Offshore & Trawl Supply (ropes) and Advanced Mooring Supply (offshore wind mooring) — plus a new UK subsidiary, moving beyond aquaculture/infra netting into offshore wind-energy mooring.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The technical-textiles field is large and growing steadily.

    Global technical textiles: ~$206B (2024) to ~$272B by 2030, ~4.8%/yr. Geosynthetics specifically: estimates range ~$18-20B (2025) to ~$32-45B by 2035, 6.1-9.5%/yr depending on source.

  • Its home niche — aquaculture netting — is smaller but faster-growing.

    Global aquaculture: ~$334-359B (2025) to ~$418-425B by 2030 (~5-6%/yr); the cage-net sub-segment specifically is projected to $2.3B by 2030 at 8.5%/yr, outpacing the broader aquaculture market.

  • A clean market-share percentage isn't the right number here — this is a fragmented, regionally-won market, not one with a single addressable-market denominator Garware competes for as a whole.

    Garware's real position shows up as dominant regional share (90% Canada, 80% Scotland) rather than a global aquaculture-market percentage, since most of that $334B+ figure is fish farming spend, not netting.

  • India's own infrastructure build-out is a second, distinct demand driver for the geosynthetics side of the business.

    India geosynthetics: ~$1.5-1.63B (2023-24) to ~$2.73B by 2030 (~9-10%/yr), pulled by the National Infrastructure Pipeline (~$1.4T), PM Gati Shakti (~$1.2T), Bharatmala's 34,000+ km of highway, and new rail lines needing track-bed stabilization.

  • Sustainability pressure cuts both ways.

    Ghost-gear and marine-plastic-pollution concern is pushing biodegradable fishing-gear adoption in North America and Europe — a future substitution risk to conventional netting — but biodegradable nets are today less durable, so it hasn't displaced Garware's product yet.

A regionally dominant, patent-holding netmaker whose margin just compressed even as its underlying markets (aquaculture, infrastructure, now offshore wind) keep expanding. The capability looks ahead of what the P&L currently shows.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Two places worth asking a question, each built on something already documented.

Velocity

The offshore wind mooring line

The July 2025 acquisitions of Offshore & Trawl Supply and Advanced Mooring Supply moved Garware into offshore wind-energy mooring — a new, higher-value adjacency to its core rope/net manufacturing. It has entered a market that is only starting to build out.

Acquisitions: leveragedgrowth.in, aquafeed.co.uk.

Margin

Pricing the anti-fouling patent

Garware holds a documented patent portfolio and DSIR-recognized R&D behind its anti-fouling net technology, yet EBITDA margin compressed from ~21% to ~19% over the last year. Worth asking whether the patented performance is priced at the premium the science supports, or absorbed into a flat headline price.

Patents/DSIR: indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in. Margin: screener.in.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Garware's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Pilot one offshore-wind mooring contract end to end

Take the Norway mooring acquisitions and run one project through the full cycle — bid, deliver, service — before deciding how much capacity to commit to the line.

Margin

Test a price differential on the anti-fouling line

Take the anti-fouling ('V2') net range specifically and test a price gap against standard netting in one or two export markets, measuring whether buyers pay for the documented performance difference.
