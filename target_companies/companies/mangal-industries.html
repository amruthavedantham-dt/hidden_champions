Part 1

Real scale and real customers, on thin returns — and a corrected identity

Revenue reached ₹1,360 Cr in FY25 (+19.65% YoY), with EBITDA growth of 24.33% and net profit growth of 23.71% — genuinely strong growth. Yet ROE (0.79%) and ROCE (2.22%) are both notably thin for a manufacturer of this scale, even as the company deleveraged (borrowings down 26.06% YoY). Worth noting: FY25's ₹1,360 Cr sits well below the company's own publicly stated target of ₹3,000 Cr by 2025, set in a 2022 press release when revenue was reported at over ₹450 Cr — a real target-vs-actual gap.

It is a design-led manufacturing partner for precision components, metal fabrication, storage solutions, toolworks, and plastic components — a real, diversified industrial manufacturer, part of the Amara Raja Group (a ~$1.75-2 billion Indian conglomerate) since its 1990 founding as a 16-employee unit making sheet-metal components for Amara Raja's own batteries. It has since expanded through plastic battery containers (1998), high-tensile fasteners (2004, 15,000 MTPA capacity), and injection-moulded plastics (2016, 2021).

Its named customer base is real and independently confirmed via trade-data aggregators: Ashok Leyland, Ford, Johnson Controls, Bosch, Areva T&D, MIC Electronics, KONE, HCL, and Regen Powertech — a genuinely credible roster. Export data suggests exports are a small minority of overall revenue (an estimated $17.1M cumulative export value against ~$160M FY25 revenue), and top export markets by trade-data are Spain, Sweden, and Nigeria.

The identity correction is the central finding of this profile: the HVT sheet's website (emis.cn), sector ('Information Services'), and segment_ref ('emerging-market-business-intelligence') are all confirmed contamination from an unrelated global business-intelligence data vendor. The real company's moat claim — AS9100D certification for high-tensile fasteners and precision machined components for Aerospace & Defence — is consistent with a real 2024-era strategic MOU with AXISCADES (a technology-solutions company) explicitly targeting aerospace, defense, medical, and heavy-engineering market entry, though the AS9100D certification itself and the '1,533 people skilled' DSIR Development Centre claim could not be independently corroborated beyond this partnership signal.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A real, growing India defence/aerospace market — though the company's actual current revenue is overwhelmingly automotive/industrial, not aerospace, today.

    India Aerospace & Defence market: $27.1B (2024) to $54.4B by 2033 (6.99%/yr); India's defense production reached a record ₹1.27 lakh crore in FY2023-24 under 'Make in India' — a real structural tailwind for the AXISCADES-partnership-driven aerospace push, which remains an emerging line, not an established one.

  • MTAR Technologies and Azad Engineering show what a mature aerospace/defence-precision player's returns look like — a real, informative contrast to Mangal's current 0.79% ROE.

    MTAR Technologies posted ₹876.2 Cr FY26 revenue (+29.6% YoY) with 12.1% ROE and 17.2% ROCE; Azad Engineering posted ₹603 Cr FY26 revenue (+32% YoY) with 8.89% ROE and 36.9% EBITDA margin — both smaller in revenue than Mangal but earning dramatically better returns, illustrating the margin upside available if Mangal's aerospace push matures.

  • A real, funded corporate parent (Amara Raja Group, ~$1.75-2B) is a genuine structural advantage most standalone manufacturers in this batch don't have.

    Group backing provides both scale and cross-selling relationships (Mangal's own founding customer was Amara Raja's battery division), a real advantage in funding a longer-horizon push like the aerospace/defence pivot.

A large, real, group-backed manufacturer whose returns don't yet match its scale — and whose source data was so badly contaminated (wrong website, wrong sector, wrong segment) that correcting the identity was the single most important task in this profile. The AXISCADES-driven aerospace push is a real, credible long-horizon bet; whether it moves the needle on the current thin ROE/ROCE is the question worth tracking.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Market-Access

Maturing the AXISCADES aerospace/defence partnership from MOU to real revenue

The HVT sheet's own gap research names potential in advanced analytics/predictive insights for manufacturing via the AXISCADES relationship — the more concrete near-term version of this is converting the confirmed strategic MOU into actual qualified aerospace/defence supply revenue, which is where peers like MTAR and Azad earn dramatically better returns than Mangal's current automotive-dominated mix.

HVT gap research; Aviation Defence Universe.

Possibilities, not prescriptions — and the honest first step here is confirming the AS9100D certification status directly.
