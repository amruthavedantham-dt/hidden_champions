Devendra Autocom: a 60-year export house that rebuilt itself around Indian Army tank engines.

A 1965 export-led fuel-injection parts maker that flipped from ~85% export to ~90% domestic, with defence now roughly half its revenue — while growing 43% YoY and improving margins.

Part 1

Strong growth, thin margins, a real structural pivot underway

Revenue reached ₹100-101 Cr in FY25, up a striking ~43% YoY on a 1-year CAGR basis — corroborated independently by both Tofler and a trade-press interview citing the same ~₹100 Cr figure, with a company-stated target of ₹200 Cr by FY26/27 at 30%+ annual growth. EBITDA and net profit both grew even faster (+144.14% and +158.07% YoY respectively), though net margin remains thin at 1.72%. ROE (9.45%) and ROCE (10.87%) are genuinely healthy, and debt-to-equity is negative (net cash) — a real financial engine emerging.

It is a manufacturer, supplier, and exporter of automotive high-precision components, and the sole Indian distributor of Hartridge (UK) diesel test equipment and Turbo Technics (UK) turbocharger machinery — a real third-party validation-testing capability, not just manufacturing.

It holds IATF/TS 16949, ISO 9001/14001/45001, and VDA 6.3 certification, plus Lean Management, Industry 4.0, and SAP HANA systems — a genuinely modern operational stack for a 60-year-old manufacturer. It states in-house R&D drives its Flexible Manufacturing System strategy and is planning a large-scale 3D printer to cut prototype lead time to 60 days.

The company's own history shows a striking structural pivot: historically ~85% export/15% domestic through the 2010s, flipping to ~90% domestic/~10% export by 2023 as it moved toward Indian OEM and defence supply. Defence now accounts for roughly half of revenue, up from a low base — anchored by a 2018 contract to supply fuel pumps, injectors, and fuel lines to Engine Factory Avadi for Indian Army T-72/T-90 tank engines, displacing previously Russian-sourced parts and breaking into a category historically dominated by Bosch and Delphi. It won the SIDM Champion Award 2024 for Import Substitution.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A real, growing India fuel-injection market, with a structural EV headwind offset by defence/commercial-vehicle demand.

    India Fuel Injection Systems Market: $2.20B (2024) to $4.11B by 2033 (7.20%/yr); near-term demand persists and even grows from commercial vehicles, agricultural equipment, construction equipment, and defence applications even as EV transition pressures light-vehicle diesel demand.

  • Bosch dominates globally, but Lucas-TVS is the closer, better-documented Indian peer — still ~10x Devendra's scale.

    Bosch holds >44% of the global diesel fuel injection market; Lucas-TVS (JV of Lucas Industries UK and TVS Group) posted >₹1,000 Cr FY25 revenue with +11% revenue CAGR and +38% EBITDA CAGR, with >75% of revenue from in-house-developed products.

  • Defence import-substitution is a real, distinct structural theme separate from the commercial-vehicle OEM cycle — and Devendra Autocom is directly inside it.

    The Engine Factory Avadi relationship broke into a category previously dominated by Bosch/Delphi and displaced Russian-sourced parts under 'Make in India' — a structural, policy-backed theme distinct from ordinary automotive-component demand.

A genuinely well-timed structural pivot — from a low-margin, purely export-led legacy business toward a higher-value, policy-backed defence import-substitution role — showing up cleanly in the numbers: strong revenue growth, improving returns, and a net-cash balance sheet. The 3D-printing prototyping investment and the ₹200 Cr FY26/27 target both suggest management is trying to compound this, not just ride a one-time contract win.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Real momentum already documented in the company's own recent history — the question is whether it broadens beyond the initial defence win.

Customer-Diversification

Converting the Engine Factory Avadi defence relationship into a broader OEM/defence base

The company is already expanding into northern India and exploring Navy/Air Force partnerships beyond its existing Army supply relationship, alongside targeting new commercial OEMs (Mahindra, Tata Motors, Kirloskar) beyond its anchor client Ashok Leyland — a credible next step given the demonstrated import-substitution win already achieved.

motorindiaonline.in (six-decades company profile).

Possibilities, not prescriptions — and the honest first step here is confirming margin improves as defence/OEM diversification scales.

Margin

Track whether net margin expands as the defence/domestic OEM mix grows further

With net margin still thin (1.72%) despite the revenue and EBITDA surge, the clean test is whether further defence and multi-OEM diversification (beyond the initial Engine Factory Avadi win) pushes margin up toward what ROE/ROCE already suggest is achievable — confirming the pivot is a genuine margin story, not just a volume one.
