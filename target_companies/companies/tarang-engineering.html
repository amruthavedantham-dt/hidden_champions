Part 1

A capability the grid-expansion market is finally moving toward

Revenue grew 53.56% to a ₹75-100 Cr band, with EBITDA up 81.08% and PAT up 111.02% year-on-year — profit compounding faster than either metric — while ROE (8.67%) stayed positive but still modest, on a low debt-to-equity of 0.08.

It's an approved vendor for NTPC, Indian Railways/RDSO and PGCIL up to 765kV-class transformers — India's highest transmission voltage class — plus PSEB, GETCO, OPTCL and other state electricity boards, with Alstom and CGL performance certificates. This is a genuinely hard qualification bar, not a self-description.

In 2024 it received formal approval for supplying radiators for 765kV-class transformers by PGCIL specifically — a dated, checkable regulatory milestone, alongside conformance to IEEMA 9:1990, DIN 42559 and EN 50216 standards.

It manufactures pressed-steel, hot-dip galvanized and stainless-steel radiators for power and distribution transformers, processing roughly 600 tonnes of steel monthly across three facilities (two in Hyderabad, one in Nagpur/Butibori), and exports to Dubai, Indonesia, Malaysia, Oman, Japan, Australia and Saudi Arabia — including a historic 3,600-radiator bulk order to Algeria (2016).

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The core field is genuinely niche, with data that varies a lot by source.

    Transformer radiator market: $1.85B (2024) to $3.65B by 2033 (8.9%/yr) per one estimate, $2.8B (2025) to $4.7B by 2034 (5.9%/yr) per another — this session's research flags it as one of the least data-consistent niches profiled in this project.

  • The demand it actually rides is the much larger, better-documented transformer/grid-expansion market.

    India power & distribution transformer market: $4.50B (2025) to $6.37B by 2031 (5.81%/yr), directly driven by India's substation capacity build-out (113,013 MVA added in 2025-26 alone at 220kV+).

  • No named radiator-specific peer with public financials exists — this is a genuinely fragmented, private-market niche.

    Named India competitors (Bharath Radiators, STC Radiators, Electronet Energy Radiators, Thermocool Engineering) have no disclosed revenue or margin figures; global 'key players' named in market reports (ABB, Siemens Energy, CG Power) are transformer OEMs, not radiator specialists.

A 765kV-qualified radiator maker in a niche too fragmented to benchmark against named peers, riding demand from a much larger and better-documented grid-expansion market. The qualification itself — not scale — is the real moat here.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One place named in the company's own gap research.

Margin

Bringing automotive-sector automation to electrical component manufacturing

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: the company's director brings automotive-sector expertise (safety/testing/homologation/NPD) into an electrical-components manufacturing business that hasn't yet widely adopted automotive-grade automation — an unusual cross-industry technology-transfer opportunity.

HVT gap research (indiamart.com, citing an unrelated third-party's CEO profile).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Tarang Engineering's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.
