Part 1

A real export operation, currently working through a genuine loss

This is a real, concerning financial reversal: FY24 revenue was ₹72.2 Cr (₹50-75 Cr band, roughly flat per Tracxn's -1% CAGR note), but the most recent Tofler snapshot (FY25) shows total revenue down 15.87% YoY, EBITDA down 41.96%, and net profit down 65.05% — with the company swinging to negative ROE (-12.82%), negative ROCE (-17.71%), and negative net margin (-4.11%). Debt-to-equity remains low (0.03) and net worth still grew 9.46%, but the earnings trend itself is genuinely negative, not just imprecise.

It describes itself as a global leader in engineering products for automobiles, mining, construction, and consumer durables. What's independently and directly confirmed on the company's own site: 95% of production is exported to 23+ countries, backed by a Pennsylvania, USA warehouse enabling same-day delivery to US/Canada customers — a genuine, verifiable export infrastructure investment, not just a self-claim.

Two specific claims in the HVT sheet could not be corroborated this session: the company's own homepage describes itself generically as 'ISO and IATF-Certified' but does not name ISO 9001:2015/IATF 16949/ISO 14001 specifically, nor mention HAL (Hindustan Aeronautics) vendor status anywhere; and no mention of a Comer Industries USA customer relationship was found on the company's own materials or any third-party source. Both should be treated as unverified pending direct confirmation, not repeated as fact.

It has been recognized with SME Achiever Awards (2015-16 and 2019-20) and among the 'Top 100 MSMEs in India' (2019) — consistent with the HVT sheet's cited award, though these are historical recognitions, not recent (last ~2 years) developments. No 2024-2025 expansion, funding, or new-contract news was found beyond an Independence Day plant celebration.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A genuinely growing forging/precision-machining market, though India-specific size estimates vary meaningfully by scope.

    India forging market: $5.21B (2024) to $10.17B by 2033 (7.71%/yr) per one IMARC estimate, or a narrower $3.36B (2024) to $6.01B by 2033 (6.18%/yr) per another IMARC scope — not directly reconcilable.

  • Larger listed peers (Sansera Engineering) show what strong execution looks like in this segment — a real contrast to LNM's current negative returns.

    Sansera Engineering posts a ~17.2% EBITDA margin with management targeting '20-20-20' (20% growth/20% EBITDA margin/20% ROCE) — vastly larger in scale than LNM but illustrative of the segment's achievable return profile when execution goes well.

  • A genuine US-warehouse export infrastructure investment is a real differentiator most small Indian forging houses don't have.

    The Pennsylvania warehouse enabling same-day US/Canada delivery is a concrete, capital-intensive commitment to export-market service levels — a real structural advantage, independent of the current earnings dip.

Constraints, read as opportunities

Diagnosis

Diagnose the FY25 revenue and profit decline before pursuing new customer acquisition
