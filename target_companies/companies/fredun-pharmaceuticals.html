Part 1

A wealth-creation engine

Revenue jumped 40% year-on-year to ₹639 Cr (FY26), EBITDA rose 72% to ₹94.79 Cr (margin expanding 276 basis points to 14.83%), and PAT climbed 60% to ₹33.21 Cr — three-way alignment (revenue, margin and profit all improving together), not just a top-line story.

Return on equity is 15.8%, ROCE 21.5% — both healthy — and the 5-year trend is even more striking: profit CAGR of 75.2% against a 37% sales CAGR, meaning profit has been compounding roughly twice as fast as revenue.

The business is diversified beyond core pharmaceuticals into nutraceuticals, cosmeceuticals, dietary supplements and animal healthcare, manufactured at a WHO-GMP certified facility in Palghar with 400+ active global product registrations.

Per the HVT sheet's own gap research, approximately 80-90% of revenue comes from international markets (Africa, Southeast Asia, CIS countries, Latin America) — a structural hedge against India's domestic drug-price-capping regulation.

Recent moves back the growth story with real capital allocation: Palghar facility expansion, a credit-rating upgrade to IVR BBB+, a new hormone-therapy range and "DAULCÉL" wellness platform launch, plus a board-approved 2:1 bonus share issue.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and growing at a healthy clip.

    Pharmaceutical contract manufacturing/CDMO estimates range ~$200-260B+ depending on scope, at 6.9-8.2%/yr — Fredun's own 37% 5-year sales CAGR is running well ahead of the category average.

  • Regulatory approval gates entry, and Fredun clears the baseline bar.

    WHO-GMP certification is the entry-level gate for regulated/semi-regulated export markets — Fredun holds it, though it hasn't (per this research pass) added the deeper USFDA/EU-GMP tier that peers like Gracure carry.

  • Against listed peers, Fredun's combination of growth and margin stands out.

    Windlas Biotech grew 20.65% with 12.4% ROE; Marksans Pharma is far larger (₹2,951 Cr) with 15.5% ROE. Fredun's 40% revenue growth and 15.8% ROE beat Windlas's growth rate while matching Marksans's ROE at a fraction of the scale.

  • Its export concentration is a deliberate structural choice, not incidental.

    80-90% of revenue from international markets explicitly avoids India's domestic price-capping regulation for pharmaceuticals — a structural margin protection most domestic-focused peers don't have.

A small-cap growing revenue, margin and profit together, at rates ahead of its listed peers, built almost entirely on exports that sidestep domestic price controls. The India-facing part of the business is the smaller story here.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Margin

Higher margins in emerging export markets

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: the export-led model already mitigates domestic risk, but the company has room to pursue higher-margin positioning specifically in emerging markets rather than treating all export revenue the same.

HVT gap research (bitget.com).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Fredun's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Margin

Segment export revenue by market margin

Break the 80-90% export revenue down by country/region for one quarter, and identify which 2-3 markets already carry the highest margin — then test whether dossier-filing effort can be weighted toward more of those markets.
