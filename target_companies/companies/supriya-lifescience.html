Part 1

A wealth-creation engine

Revenue grew from ₹570 Cr (FY24) to ₹828 Cr (FY26), and PAT from ₹119 Cr to ₹209 Cr over the same stretch — both growing, EBITDA margin holding around 35-36% throughout.

Return on equity is 19.1%, return on capital employed 25.1% — a healthy balance-sheet return, not just a top-line growth story.

It was the first Indian company to receive Brazilian ANVISA approval for Esketamine Hydrochloride, and separately developed a low-cost manufacturing route for Atorvastatin — differentiation the company itself flagged in a regulatory filing, not yet independently corroborated beyond that filing and trade press.

Regulatory depth backs the claim: three USFDA approvals, two EU-GMP/EDQM certifications, plus WHO-GMP, Korean FDA, Mexican FDA, TGA Australia, PMDA Japan, NMPA China, Health Canada and ANVISA Brazil GMP — 21 US DMFs submitted and 11 CEPs granted by EDQM as of December 2025.

It's committing ₹510 Cr to new capacity: ₹160 Cr at Ambernath (commercial operations targeted early 2026) and ₹350 Cr at Patalganga (phased over 2.5-3 years), funded from internal accruals, with a stated target of ₹1,600 Cr revenue within three years — roughly double the current run rate.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large, and estimates diverge by research house.

    Global API market: Grand View Research puts it at $359.45B by 2030 (5.85%/yr); MarketsandMarkets at $198.39B by 2030 (6.6%/yr); a third estimate lands at $345.5B by 2030 (6.2%/yr). Overall range ~$232-360B by 2030.

  • India's own API market is smaller but growing faster than the global average.

    India API: $13.60B (2024) to $21.99B by 2030, 8.30%/yr — faster than the ~6% global rate, backed by a ₹6,940 Cr PLI scheme covering 35 critical APIs that once accounted for ~67% of India's import dependence.

  • The China+1 story is real but not one-directional.

    China controls ~80% of global generic-drug API supply, and India itself still sources roughly two-thirds of its own API needs (70-90% of KSMs/basic intermediates) from China — so India's China+1 positioning carries its own upstream dependency, not a clean substitution story.

  • Against listed peers, Supriya is the smallest of a size-differentiated set — and the closest-matched one is doing well.

    Divi's Labs (₹9,360 Cr) and Laurus Labs (₹5,554 Cr) are considerably larger; Neuland Laboratories, the closest scale peer, posted ₹1,477 Cr revenue with 22% EBITDA margin and ~20% ROE in FY25 — Supriya's own 19.1% ROE sits in a similar range despite roughly half Neuland's revenue.

  • Buyer qualification, not price, gates entry.

    A 12-24+ month audit/qualification cycle typically precedes a new API sourcing relationship; regulatory filing depth (DMF/CEP count, certifications held) is what determines whether a formulator can even consider a supplier.

A profitable, regulator-approved exporter in a market growing faster in India than globally, now spending its own cash to roughly double capacity. The bet is on filing depth converting into share, not on the market doing the work alone.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Two places worth watching, both already funded and in motion.

Velocity

Turning the ANVISA approval into Latin American share

The HVT research itself flags this: the ANVISA approval for Esketamine Hydrochloride is a step, not yet a realized market position, in Brazil and the wider Latin American region. Latin America is currently just 8% of Supriya's export mix, well behind Asia and Europe at 42% each.

HVT gap research (company filing). Export mix: businessfocusmanufacturing.com.

Margin

The ₹510 Cr capacity bet doubling revenue without doubling headcount

Ambernath and Patalganga are being funded from internal accruals toward a stated ₹1,600 Cr revenue target within three years. If the new capacity runs at anything close to the existing 35-36% EBITDA margin, the incremental revenue carries disproportionate profit.

Capex and target: Outlook Business.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Supriya's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Target one Latin American distributor relationship first

Rather than a broad regional push, identify one or two distributors in Brazil already active in Esketamine's therapeutic category and test a direct commercial relationship before building out wider Latin American coverage.

Margin

Track margin at Ambernath from first commercial batch

As Ambernath comes online in early 2026, track its unit economics separately from day one rather than blending into consolidated figures, to confirm the new capacity actually holds the 35-36% margin before Patalganga's larger spend follows the same playbook.
