Part 1

Genuinely strong returns, inside a group lineage with an unresolved identity wrinkle

FY25 revenue reached ₹70.53 Cr (+21% YoY); an earlier FY22 filing shows ROE of 20.23% and ROCE of 19.94% — genuinely healthy returns for a manufacturer, though the most recent PAT/EBITDA absolute figures are paywalled and this specific return data point is now a few years dated. EBITDA and net profit both grew explosively in FY22 (+391.16%, +642.8% YoY) off a small base, and debt-to-equity remains low at 0.06.

It manufactures and exports cast iron and ductile iron pump components, pipe fittings, and municipal castings with N9 & N10 surface finish, and runs an in-house R&D department and casting/machining facility explicitly aimed at reducing lead time and developing new products.

It holds ISO 9001:2008 and ISO 45001:2018 certification, with its works also TÜV Germany-audited — a genuine third-party quality signal beyond self-certification. It has produced lost-foam castings up to 120 kg and 750mm max component size, at an annual volume of 1,800 metric tons, and won an EEPC India Export Excellence Award plus 2nd Prize in the Casting Category at the 2023-24 KBL (Kirloskar Brothers) Vendor Meet, alongside quality recognitions from Shakti Pump and KSB.

The company's own website states a founding date of 2014 tied to CIN U27320PN2017PTC172131, while Tracxn lists a different CIN (U27310PN2014PTC151576) for what appears to be the same company and website — this discrepancy was flagged by HVT's own research and is not resolved here; the Tracxn CIN is used in this profile as HVT's own research judgment, not as a confirmed fact.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A large, fast-growing India foundry market, with pump castings as a specialized, quality-gated sub-segment.

    India Foundry and Casting Market: $22.0B (2025) to $57.9B by 2034 (10.78%/yr); India's global casting export share is historically ~2-3%, with the Ministry of Heavy Industries targeting a doubling to 6-7%.

  • Named downstream pump-OEM customers are a genuinely strong, independently plausible customer list, not just self-claimed positioning.

    Siddharth Foundry names Shakti Pumps, Kirloskar Brothers (KBL), KSB Ltd, DAB Pumps, Goulds Pumps, SPX Flow Technology, ITT Corporation, Atlas Copco, and Mather & Platt Pumps as customers — corroborated in part by the documented KBL Vendor Meet award and Shakti Pump/KSB quality recognitions.

  • Larger integrated foundry peers exist (Kirloskar Ferrous, Nelcast, Electrosteel) but none with directly comparable financial disclosure found this pass.

    Kirloskar Ferrous Industries explicitly manufactures pump castings and cylinder-block castings; Nelcast and Electrosteel Castings are cited as major foundry competitors, though revenue/margin/ROE figures for these peers were not retrieved this pass.

A genuinely strong operator by the numbers — real ROE, a corroborated OEM award history, and a third-party-audited quality system — sitting inside a corporate-identity wrinkle (the CIN mismatch between the company's own site and Tracxn) that should be resolved directly before treating any single data source here as fully authoritative.

Market data: IMARC Group, Market.us (shared pump-cast-components segment file). Peer data: Kirloskar Ferrous, Nelcast, Electrosteel Castings, Bharat Foundry.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Product-Expansion

Scaling lost-foam casting capability for complex geometries

The HVT sheet's own gap research names the lost-foam casting process — currently proven up to 120 kg / 750mm components at 1,800 MT/year — as having further, not-yet-fully-detailed potential for complex geometries and broader market penetration, a credible lever given the process is already operational, not aspirational.

HVT gap research (siddharthfoundry.com lost-foam page).

Possibilities, not prescriptions — and the honest first step here is resolving the CIN discrepancy before anything else.
