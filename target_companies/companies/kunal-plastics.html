Part 1

A real, if thin-margin, business — about to change corporate form entirely

As a standalone entity, Kunal Plastics showed revenue in the ₹75-100 Cr band for FY25 (+10.75% YoY), with net profit growing sharply (+50.9% YoY) though ROE remained thin at 3.98%. It fully repaid its borrowings (-100% YoY), moving to a net-cash position — a real, if modest, financial improvement pre-merger. Note that Tofler's auto-generated business description for this CIN describes 'planters, square plastic pots, nursery planters, and hanging baskets' — this is almost certainly a mis-mapped generic NIC industry code, not a real reflection of the business, which the company's own site and this session's research confirm is hygiene packaging for baby care, sanitary, food, and medical supplies.

It specializes in hygiene packaging solutions for baby care, sanitary, food, and medical supplies, and is actively exploring incorporation of Post-Industrial Recycled (PIR) and Post-Consumer Recycled (PCR) plastics into its hygiene packaging — a real, regulation-aligned direction given India's April 2025 mandate for minimum 30% recycled content in PET bottles (targeting 60% by 2029).

This is the central and most consequential finding of this profile: Kunal Plastics is being formally amalgamated into listed Ecoplast Limited. NCLT Ahmedabad approved the merger following a joint scheme application filed by both companies in July 2025 and shareholder/creditor meetings in November 2025, at a swap ratio of 52 Ecoplast shares (₹10 face value) for every 1 Kunal Plastics share (₹100 face value). Ecoplast completed the allotment of 13,00,000 new shares on 15 June 2026, finalizing the amalgamation — meaning Kunal Plastics, as a standalone private company, effectively ceases to exist as a separate entity going forward. This explains why the company's content is hosted directly on Ecoplast's own website (ecoplastindia.com).

The combined/parent entity (Ecoplast Limited, listed) posted FY26 revenue of ₹221 Cr with PAT of ₹12 Cr, ROE 11%, and ROCE 14.4% — materially healthier returns than Kunal Plastics showed standalone, and the combined entity is now pursuing a ₹12.75 Cr capacity expansion (adding 1,800 MT extrusion and 1,680 MT coating capacity by end-2026), with current capacity already running at 100% utilization. Ecoplast is also India's first Surface Protection Film (SPF) manufacturer with AEO certification, though this certification's applicability to Kunal Plastics' own hygiene-packaging line specifically was not confirmed.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A genuinely above-market-growth hygiene-packaging niche, sitting inside a slower-growing broader plastic packaging market.

    India's overall plastic packaging market grows at 3.06%/yr, while the India baby diaper market (a core hygiene-packaging end use) grows at 6.37%/yr (2026-2034) — hygiene packaging is a genuine above-market-growth sub-segment.

  • UFlex is the dominant named India peer, at a scale far beyond what Kunal Plastics/Ecoplast combined represent.

    UFlex is described as India's largest multinational flexible packaging company, with strong export presence across North America, Europe, Africa, SE Asia, and the Middle East — no revenue figure was independently found this pass, but its scale is understood to be materially larger.

  • The merger itself is a real structural upgrade — moving from a private, thin-ROE entity into a listed company with public capital-market access.

    Ecoplast's post-merger ₹12.75 Cr capacity expansion plan, funded as a listed entity, represents a meaningfully different growth-funding capability than Kunal Plastics had as a standalone private company with only 3.98% ROE.

The honest story here isn't really about Kunal Plastics as a standalone company anymore — it's about a 42-year hygiene-packaging specialist being absorbed into a listed entity right as this profile was researched, converting a thin-margin private business into a division with real public capital-market access. Whatever growth trajectory follows should now be tracked at the Ecoplast level, not as Kunal Plastics standalone.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Capability-Build

Scaling PIR/PCR recycled-content incorporation with Ecoplast's post-merger capacity and capital access

The exploration of recycled-content packaging, combined with India's regulatory mandate trajectory (30% recycled PET content from April 2025, 60% by 2029) and Ecoplast's newly-funded capacity expansion, is a credible near-term lever now that the merger provides public capital-market access this standalone business didn't have.

Possibilities, not prescriptions — and the honest first step here is re-profiling this business at the Ecoplast (post-merger) level going forward.

Trust

Since the merger is now complete (shares allotted 15 June 2026), any future financial or strategic tracking of this hygiene-packaging business should reference Ecoplast Limited's consolidated results and disclosures, not Kunal Plastics' now-superseded standalone filings.
