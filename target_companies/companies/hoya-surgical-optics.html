Part 1

A capability the India margin hasn't caught up to

India-entity revenue grew 18.72% to a ₹100-150 Cr band in FY25, with net profit up 49.93% YoY and EBITDA up 66.7% YoY — but off a thin base, with net margin just 1.05% and ROE 3.35%, on a debt-free balance sheet (D/E 0.0).

The parent company's global position is real and independently checkable: HOYA's own 2025 investor materials claim 3rd-largest global IOL market share (an independent industry directory instead ranks it 4th — both cited), driven by its Vivinex/multiSert preloaded lens platform.

It holds FDA approval for commercial distribution of clear foldable hydrophobic acrylic IOLs — third-party sourced (ophthalmology trade press, not the company's own site), the strongest-credibility moat claim in this profile.

R&D runs from two centers — Japan (legacy) and a newer global center in Singapore for new IOL products and technologies — positioned directly against the growing cataract burden, especially in Asia, per the HVT sheet's own alignment research.

At the parent level, Life Care (the vision-care segment HOYA Surgical Optics sits inside) makes up 64% of HOYA Corporation's total group revenue as of FY ending March 2025 — this is not a side business for the parent, it's the core of the company.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and growing steadily, with estimates diverging moderately by methodology.

    Global IOL market: $4.46B (2024) to $7.16B by 2033 on the conservative end; up to $7.34B (2026) to $10.09B by 2031 on a more bullish estimate.

  • India's own cataract burden shows real unmet demand, not a saturated market.

    Historical data shows annual cataract-blindness incidence of ~3.8 million against annual surgical throughput of only ~1.6-1.9 million operations — a persistent capacity gap, not a mature, fully-served market.

  • The preloaded-injector sub-segment is the fastest-growing part of the category, and it's exactly HOYA's own product line.

    Preloaded IOL injector systems are projected to capture ~72% revenue share of the injector market by 2026, driven by sterility and operational efficiency — directly aligned with HOYA's own preloaded-lens positioning.

  • Named global competitors (Alcon, J&J Vision, Bausch + Lomb, Carl Zeiss) operate at comparable or larger scale, though segment-specific figures are mostly undisclosed.

    Bausch + Lomb's premium-IOL revenue grew 20% YoY in Q4 2025 — one of the few peers with a disclosed IOL-specific growth figure.

A top-tier global IOL maker's India operation, growing fast but still thin on margin, inside a parent whose Life Care segment is genuinely its core business, not a diversification. India's own capacity gap suggests real room to grow regardless of how the global share numbers are counted.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One place worth watching, grounded in India's own documented capacity gap.

Velocity

Closing India's cataract-surgery capacity gap

India's own historical data shows annual cataract-blindness incidence outpacing surgical throughput by roughly 2x — a structural, documented demand gap that a global-scale IOL/injector maker with dual R&D centers is positioned to help close as India's surgical capacity expands.

Cataract burden data: frontiersin.org/IJO literature aggregation.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only HOYA Surgical Optics India's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Partner with one high-volume surgical program

Identify one high-volume cataract-surgery provider (e.g. a large eye-hospital network) and deepen the supply relationship specifically around India's capacity gap, using it as a template before a broader distribution push.
