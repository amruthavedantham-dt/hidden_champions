Part 1

Real decades, real customers, thin margins

Revenue sits in the ₹75-100 Cr band (FY24, +7.41% YoY), but profitability is razor-thin: net profit margin of just 0.08%, ROE 0.62%, ROCE 1.75%. What stands out is the growth trajectory rather than the absolute level — EBITDA grew 23.25% YoY and net profit surged 82.01% YoY, both off a small base, with debt-to-equity at -0.08 (net cash).

It manufactures mechanical power presses (C Frame, H Frame, High-Speed, Permanent Inclined) and shearing machines, describing itself as having '50+ years of engineering excellence' — a claim consistent with the company's own founding narrative (1971) even though the legal entity's CIN dates to 1983.

It holds ISO 9001:2015 certification and names Yamaha, TTK, Havells, Hawkins, and JBM Group as customers — a genuinely impressive roster if accurate, though the HVT sheet itself flags this `moat` claim `needs_verify: yes` since it rests entirely on the company's own website with no independent confirmation.

It operates a joint venture, VeeGee Kaushico Engineering Pvt. Ltd., for sheet-metal component manufacturing, with current leadership under Managing Director Mridul Sharma (son of founder R.S. Sharma) — a real multi-generational family succession. Its MCA-filed employee count of 18 is strikingly small for a company of this age and revenue scale, most plausibly reflecting only statutory/office headcount rather than the full shop-floor workforce.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A large, growing metal-stamping demand base in India, with mechanical presses still the dominant press type.

    India metal stamping market: $4.65B (2023), 5.7-6.3%/yr depending on report vintage; mechanical presses held the largest revenue share within the press-machine market at 52.3% in 2023, favored for cost-effectiveness in uniform, straightforward-shape parts.

  • A real structural shift toward servo-electric presses is underway — a genuine long-term threat to the mechanical-press category Kaushico specializes in.

    Servo press segment is cited as the fastest-growing press-type segment going forward (India-specific servo-press growth cited at 6.1%/yr), driven by energy efficiency, programmable stroke profiles, and higher repeatability.

  • No named Indian mechanical-power-press peer with disclosed financials exists to benchmark Kaushico's thin margins against.

    General directories list numerous Indian power-press manufacturers, but none with disclosed revenue/margin/ROE — Kaushico's own 50-year leadership claim is self-positioned, not independently verified against named competitors.

A genuinely old, customer-credentialed manufacturer whose margins simply haven't kept pace with its longevity or its claimed customer roster. The servo-electric shift is a real structural risk to watch — Kaushico's own gap research already names smart/connected-machine features as the response, but hasn't yet shown up in the numbers.

Market data: Grand View Research (shared mechanical-power-presses segment file). No independently financialed Indian peer was found for direct comparison.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Product-Expansion

Adding Industry 4.0 features to an aging mechanical-press line

The HVT sheet's own gap research names smart, connected machines with real-time production data, predictive maintenance, remote monitoring, and energy optimization as the credible response to the segment's servo-electric shift — a real lever given Kaushico's existing R&D investment, though one that needs to move the needle on the current 0.08% net margin to matter.
