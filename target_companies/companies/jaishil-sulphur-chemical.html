Part 1

A capability with a genuine, checkable certification behind it

Financials here are thin: the only revenue figure found is an aggregator's estimate (~$5.3M), self-claimed tier, with no PAT, EBITDA margin or ROE available — expected for a partnership firm without ROC filing obligations, but a real limit on how much can be confirmed.

One claim genuinely clears independent verification: Jaishil is OMRI-listed (Organic Materials Review Institute), a real third-party organic-input registry, not a self-issued certificate — a meaningfully stronger credibility tier than most of this company's other claims.

It claims 50,000+ MT/annum capacity across six India locations (Navi Mumbai, Surat, Gandhidham, Vizag, Kolkata, Chennai), a government CIR registration (CIR-32624/2000) for its Sulphur 80% WDG product distributed by UPL Sustainable Agri Solutions, and India's first low-dosage zinc fertilizer (NutriZin 700) — all self-claimed, and the HVT sheet flags all three pillars (`differentiation`, `moat`, `product_improvement`) as unresolved despite `Verified` review status.

Founding year is also genuinely uncertain: the company's own materials say 1979, but a separate aggregator lists a 2013 incorporation with a CIN that, on inspection, appears to belong to a different, unrelated Haryana-registered entity — treated here as not reliably confirmed rather than picked between.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is modest-sized but growing steadily, anchored to a real, documented deficiency.

    India micronutrient fertilizer market: $0.81B (2025) to $1.12B by 2030 (6.70%/yr); zinc specifically grows faster (8.24%/yr) and was already the largest micronutrient category by consumption share in India in 2022 (26.9%).

  • The deficiency driving demand is structural and geographically documented, not a marketing claim.

    An estimated 36.5% of Indian soils are zinc-deficient per ICAR data, reaching as high as 60% in some states.

  • Government subsidy policy is a direct tailwind.

    The Nutrient-Based Subsidy scheme now incorporates Magnesium/Zinc/Boron/Sulphur-fortified NPK as of Kharif 2024; Andhra Pradesh has already run a state-level micronutrient subsidy model using Soil Health Card data that other states may replicate.

  • The closest listed comparable shows healthier margins than Jaishil's own figures suggest for itself.

    Aries Agro Limited (broader micronutrient/animal-nutrition player, not sulphur-specialist) posted ₹957 Cr revenue (FY26, +18.93%) at 9.29% EBITDA margin, 13.7% ROE — a useful structural benchmark, though not a size-comparable peer to Jaishil.

A real organic certification and a documented soil-deficiency tailwind sit behind a company whose own scale and history can't be independently pinned down. The certification is the one thing here that doesn't need to be taken on faith.

Market data: Mordor Intelligence, GMInsights, ICAR (via crops.zinc.org). Sulphur Mills Limited was identified as the closest direct competitor but has no public financials (private company).
