Part 1

A wealth-creation engine, mid-expansion

Revenue reached ₹425-433 Cr in FY25 with PAT around ₹58 Cr, and the growth has continued into FY26 — H1 revenue up 29% to ₹211 Cr, H1 PAT up 41% to ₹29.31 Cr. EBITDA margin sits in a healthy 17-21% range and ROE at 18% (3-year average 25%) — a genuinely strong, broad-based financial picture.

It holds a real, checkable first: India's first all-India BIS license for ester-liquid-filled distribution transformers up to 2500 kVA under IS 1180: Part 3: 2021 — a specific, verifiable regulatory milestone rather than a vague scale claim.

It's mid-way through a three-phase capacity expansion: Phase 1 (completed October 2025) took capacity from 4,681 MVA to roughly 8,500 MVA; Phase 2 targets ~11,000 MVA by December 2025 with new 220kV capability; Phase 3 land has already been acquired. Alongside this, it's investing ~₹20 Cr in backward integration into a sheet-metal fabrication subsidiary.

It raised ₹197.9 Cr in an October 2024 IPO on NSE Emerge, oversubscribed 123 times — real market validation — and CRISIL upgraded its rating to A-/Stable in March 2026, a four-notch improvement over three years. Order book stood at ₹390-400 Cr as of late 2025.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and growing steadily, with India's own segment outpacing the global rate.

    India power & distribution transformer market: $4.50B (2025) to $6.37B by 2031, 5.81%/yr; the narrower distribution-transformer sub-segment is growing faster still, 8.98%/yr.

  • India's own grid-expansion targets are the direct structural driver.

    India added 113,013 MVA of substation capacity at the 220kV+ level in 2025-26 alone, and targets 500 GW of renewable capacity by 2030 — both requiring exactly the kind of transformer manufacturing Danish Power is expanding into.

  • Listed peers show this is a genuinely strong-margin segment when well-run, not just a Danish Power story.

    Voltamp Transformers posted record ₹2,153.69 Cr revenue with 18.96% average ROE; Indo Tech Transformers posted 32% latest ROE — Danish Power's own 18-25% ROE range sits credibly within this peer set.

  • Export share is still modest but explicitly targeted to grow fast.

    Current export contribution is 8-10% of revenue, with company targets of ~20% by FY27 and ~30% by FY28 — the HVT sheet's own gap research names doubling export contribution to 20% by FY27 directly.

A genuinely strong operator by every financial measure, mid-way through the exact capacity expansion its export and domestic-demand targets require, backed by real capital markets validation. This is one of the strongest financial profiles in this entire project.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, already targeted with a specific number by the company itself.

Velocity

Doubling export contribution to 20% by FY27

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: the company has stated a specific target of doubling export contribution from 8-10% today to 20% by FY27, with ~30% targeted by FY28 — a concrete, self-imposed benchmark to track rather than a vague ambition.

HVT gap research (x.com/LnprCapital coverage).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Danish Power's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Pursue UL certification to unlock the US market

Given the US market is currently blocked purely by lack of UL certification (not a capability gap), pursuing that specific certification is a clearly-scoped, trackable step toward the export target, distinct from broader Europe/Middle East/Africa expansion.
