Part 1

A deep-tech bet across a group whose full shape isn't public

The one entity with actual filed financials — Comprotech Engineering, the flagship legal entity behind the HVT row's own website — shows real strain: revenue in a ₹50-75 Cr band for FY25, PAT down 33.88% YoY, ROE -3.69%, even as EBITDA grew 12.71%. This is not the whole group's financial picture; no consolidated figure exists.

The HVT sheet's own gap research states a group-wide order book of ₹1,500 crore over the next three years and expected revenue crossing ₹500 crore this year — self-claimed, and roughly 7-10x the one filed financial figure available (Comprotech alone), consistent with a genuinely multi-entity group rather than a single company understating its size.

It carries AS9100C certification for high-precision engineering components and mechanical sub-assemblies, and its named customer list is a genuine tier-one aerospace roster: Rolls-Royce, GE Aviation, Honeywell, Boeing, Safran, Collins Aerospace, plus domestic PSU/DRDO customers (DRDL, BEL, HAL Koraput) — a joint venture with ITP Group (a Rolls-Royce subsidiary, Spain) covers aircraft-engine-component manufacturing specifically.

In 2025-2026 it opened a new >₹100 Cr, 25,000 sq ft 'DeepTech' design-to-integration facility at Hyderabad's Hardware Park, unveiling six indigenous UAV/autonomous-defence product categories — loitering munitions, decoy drones, target drones, tethered surveillance drones, micro turbojet engines, and unmanned ground vehicles — with MOUs signed with the Indian Army, Bharat Dynamics Limited, IIIT Hyderabad and ARCI.

The founding narrative and even current leadership identity show real conflicts across sources: one profile credits G. Thrimuthulu as 2003 founder with Vamsi Vikas Ganesula (IIM Kolkata) taking over in 2004 at age 23; another names Comprotech's own 1976 founding by P.K. Reddy, with Vamsi Vikas taking over that specific entity only in 2018. A stated aspirational target ('$100 million company, ~1,000 employees') should be read as a goal, not a reported figure.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and growing across every sub-segment it touches.

    Aerospace & defense parts manufacturing: $643B-$1,003B (2024/25) growing to $961B-$1,427B by 2032-33 (5.15-5.4%/yr). Global UAV market: $26-32B (2024/25) to $41-63B by 2030 (9.2-12%/yr). India's own drone market specifically grows fastest of all: $654M (2024) to $1,437M by 2029 (17.0%/yr).

  • Government policy is a direct, well-funded tailwind for exactly the UAV bet the group just made.

    India's Union Budget 2024-25 allocated ~$68.3B to defense including ~$20.8B capital outlay; DRDO allocated ~$4B in 2024 for drone-system development; GST on military drones/critical defence equipment was fully abolished.

  • The most directly comparable listed peer shows real momentum in the exact product category.

    Azad Engineering (precision-forged aerospace/defense components) posted ₹603 Cr revenue and delivered India's first indigenous expendable turbojet engine to DRDO in 2026 — a similar product-and-customer profile to Raghu Vamsi's own turbojet/UAV ambitions, at a scale the group's stated ₹500 Cr+ target would approach.

  • AS9100/NADCAP certification is the actual gate, not scale or brand.

    AS9100 certification functions as a de facto requirement for defense-ministry/OEM approved-vendor-list status; NADCAP is process-specific and complementary, required by name for specific critical manufacturing processes by OEM customers like Safran.

A group with genuinely blue-chip aerospace customers and a real, funded UAV platform bet, whose actual consolidated size cannot currently be verified from public filings. The story here is as much about corporate structure as about the technology.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research — with a structural caveat.

Velocity

Scaling production to match the new order book

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: significant investment has gone into new facilities and R&D for UAVs and autonomous systems, but the current revenue and order book indicate room for further monetization — production scale-up, not demand generation, is the stated bottleneck.

HVT gap research (The Hindu).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Raghu Vamsi's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Take one UAV product category from prototype to first production order

Of the six unveiled UAV/autonomous-defence categories, pick the one closest to a signed MOU (e.g. with the Indian Army or BDL) and drive it specifically to a first production order, rather than scaling all six in parallel.
