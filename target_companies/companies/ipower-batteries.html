Part 1

Real certifications and OEM reach, financials that don't agree with each other

This is a genuine three-way financial conflict, not a minor discrepancy: a 2021-22 press account cites ₹50 Cr revenue (FY20-21); Tracxn shows a ₹50-100 Cr band for FY25 with a striking -62% 1-year revenue CAGR (i.e., a sharp recent decline); Tofler separately shows a ₹100-500 Cr band for FY24 with EBITDA growing +49.84% YoY (i.e., strong recent growth) — the Tracxn and Tofler pictures point in opposite directions for adjacent fiscal years. None of the three sources is dismissed here; all three are reported so the conflict is visible rather than resolved by guessing.

It develops IoT-enabled lithium-ion batteries for multi-category electric vehicles, with proprietary BMS, CAN, IoT, GPS/GPRS, and remote-monitoring capabilities — and unlike many self-claimed capability statements in this batch, the underlying certifications are independently confirmed: AIS 156 (Amendment III) Phase 2 and ICAT certification for NMC and LFP battery chemistries, plus TSEC approval for telecom batteries.

It is DSIR-registered with a documented history of developing NMC, LFP, and LMFP (Rugpro series) batteries, plus graphene-based batteries for electric two-wheelers — a registry-confirmed R&D credential, not merely self-claimed.

It supplies roughly 100 OEMs, including 15 of India's top 20 EV two-wheeler makers (Gemopai, Benling, Okinawa, Ampere, and Hero Electric named), operates 200+ service centers, and runs a Kundli, Haryana facility certified to ISO 9001/14001/45001 with a government-recognized in-house R&D centre, producing 500+ packs/day. A collaboration with e-Rise EV to install a first-of-its-kind battery service center — with staff training included — is the concrete example of this OEM-facing service model in action. No external funding round was found; cells are imported from Japan/Korea/China while other components are made in-house.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A large, genuinely fast-growing India market with a real, funded government tailwind.

    India lithium-ion battery market: $3.59B-$6.73B (2025-26) projected to reach $9.79B-$15.92B by 2030-2034 (11.78-17.65%/yr); India's PLI/ACC scheme allocates ₹18,100 Cr for 50GWh of domestic advanced-chemistry-cell capacity by 2030.

  • Certification is a must-have gate, not a nice-to-have — and Ipower already clears it, unlike many aspirants.

    OEM battery qualification requires AIS 156 Amendment III Phase 2, ICAT for NMC/LFP chemistries, and TSEC approval for telecom — Ipower holds all three, a genuine differentiator versus segment entrants still working toward certification.

  • Segment execution risk is real, even for well-funded players — a caution that makes Ipower's own unfunded, certification-led approach worth watching either way.

    Exide Industries (ROE 7.05%) and Amara Raja (ROE 14.0%) show established players can be profitable; Log9 Materials entered NCLT insolvency by Nov 2025 despite raising $56.1M — a genuine cautionary data point on capital intensity and execution risk in this segment.

The certification stack and 15-of-top-20-OEM reach are the most concrete, independently-anchored facts in this profile — genuinely hard to fake and genuinely valuable. The financial picture is the opposite: three sources that don't agree, including one showing a sharp recent decline and another showing strong recent growth. Both can't be simultaneously true, and this should be resolved directly before treating either revenue trajectory as decided.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Customer-Diversification

Scaling the battery-service-center model beyond the e-Rise EV pilot

The HVT sheet's own gap research names the e-Rise EV first-of-its-kind battery service center and staff-training collaboration as a template for a comprehensive network — a credible lever given Ipower already operates 200+ service centers and supplies ~100 OEMs, suggesting the infrastructure to scale this exists already.

HVT gap research (auto.economictimes.indiatimes.com).

Possibilities, not prescriptions — and the honest first step here is resolving the three-way revenue conflict before anything else.

Trust

Reconcile the Tracxn (-62% CAGR) vs. Tofler (+49.84% EBITDA growth) revenue conflict directly

Since these two sources point in opposite directions for adjacent fiscal years, a direct confirmation of actual current revenue trajectory with the company should precede any capacity, funding, or expansion conversation — the certification/OEM story is strong regardless, but the financial trajectory genuinely isn't known from outside sources alone.
