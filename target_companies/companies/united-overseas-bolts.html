Part 1

A real client roster, a genuinely new corporate shell

No revenue, PAT or ROE figure was found anywhere — the current legal entity is young (incorporated December 2021) and the underlying business, while older, is private and small enough to carry no public financial disclosure obligation.

Two independent sources corroborate that the actual operating business, 'United Overseas (Bolts),' dates to 2003 (founded by Rajesh Nagpal, with Sunil Bajaj cited by one source) — the 2021-incorporated private limited entity behind this HVT row's CIN is a corporate-form restructuring of that older business, not a genuinely new company. The small authorized capital (₹15 lakh) is consistent with a formalization step rather than a fresh startup.

It claims ISO 9001:2015, CE 89/106/EEC, API 20E BSL1/2/3 and ZED GOLD certification, and a monthly output over 500 metric tonnes across one large manufacturing facility plus semi-finishing facilities at five other locations around Ludhiana — all self-claimed, and the HVT sheet did not flag `moat` for verification despite the multi-certification claim resting entirely on the company's own site.

Independently-checkable trade data shows real export activity: 842 recorded shipments to 29 buyers, with Italy, the United States and Spain as primary markets — a genuine, if modest, international footprint alongside blue-chip domestic clients including Indian Railways, Indian Oil, L&T, Hindustan Petroleum, Bharat Petroleum and BHEL.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and growing, with India's own segment outpacing the global rate across every source checked.

    Global industrial fasteners: $103.92-124.2B (2025), 3.4-5.1%/yr. India industrial fasteners market: growing 6.31-8.7%/yr depending on source, consistently faster than the global average.

  • Its existing domestic client base sits in exactly the sectors driving the segment's growth.

    Infrastructure/construction growth ('Make in India,' the Smart Towns Mission) and automotive demand are the segment's cited growth drivers — United Overseas Bolts' own clients (Indian Railways, IOC, L&T, HPCL, BPCL, BHEL) span rail, energy and infrastructure directly.

  • The one named listed peer operates at a completely different order of magnitude.

    Sundram Fasteners posted ₹5,209.74 Cr FY25 revenue with ~₹540-547 Cr PAT — useful as an industry benchmark for what a mature, diversified fastener maker looks like at scale, not a size-comparable peer.

A genuinely blue-chip domestic client list and a real, checkable export footprint, sitting inside a corporate shell too new to have built its own financial track record yet. The business itself looks older and more established than its own paperwork suggests.
