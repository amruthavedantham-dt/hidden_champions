Part 1

A capability the margin hasn't caught up to

Revenue was ₹59.9 Cr in FY25, up 14.5% year-on-year, but the more striking number is profit growth: both net profit and EBITDA grew about 91% YoY off a small base — net margin is still thin at 3.35%, though ROCE (13.89%) is more solid.

It's a genuinely small, closely-held company — 93 employees as of August 2025, ₹6.25 lakh paid-up capital, directors Sonalben and Adarsh Alok Desai — with a real regulatory footprint for its size: DSIR-recognized R&D unit, ISO 9001:2008 certified, ZED Gold MSME certification.

It won India's National Startup Award (2022, Ministry of Commerce and Industry) and was runner-up for Most Innovative Company at ASSOCHAM's 9th MSME Excellence Awards (2023) — independent recognition, not just self-description.

The company claims presence in 55+ countries with 200+ enzyme products across 17+ industrial applications, but the only independently trackable export figure — a formal trade-shipment record — shows just $1.20M in tracked turnover over a full year (Dec 2024-Nov 2025), which likely undercounts total exports but is a much smaller number than the 55-country claim implies on its own.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is mid-sized and growing steadily, with a wide estimate range.

    Global industrial enzymes: $6.4-8.4B (2024/25) to $8.2-12B by 2030, 4.3-7.3%/yr depending on source.

  • India's market is smaller, faster-growing, and already concentrated.

    India enzymes: $440.5M (2023), 7.8%/yr; industrial enzymes are 54.7% of that; the top players already hold 75%+ share, meaning Infinita competes in a market where a handful of firms dominate.

  • The closest domestic peer is roughly ten times its size.

    Advanced Enzyme Technologies posted ₹637 Cr revenue (FY25) at 31% EBITDA margin and 10% ROE, present in 70+ countries — a scale-differentiated benchmark, not a direct peer, but it shows what margin looks like at scale in this category.

  • The structural tailwind is real and policy-backed.

    Enzymes are being positioned as sustainable substitutes for harsh industrial chemicals (textile bleaching, pulp/paper, biodegradable detergents); cold-water detergent adoption in North America/Europe and EU green-chemistry policy both directly favor enzyme-based processing.

A small, independently-recognized, DSIR-backed enzyme maker growing profit fast off a thin margin, in a market a tenth of Advanced Enzyme's size is already navigating at 31% margin. The gap between the two is the whole opportunity.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, visible in the gap between its export claim and its trackable trade data.

Margin

Closing the gap to Advanced Enzyme's margin

Advanced Enzyme Technologies runs at 31% EBITDA margin at ~10x Infinita's scale. Infinita's net margin is 3.35% despite fast profit growth — worth asking how much of that gap is scale economics that capacity growth alone would close, versus pricing/mix questions independent of scale.

Advanced Enzyme figures: screener.in. Infinita figures: Tofler.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Infinita's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Margin

Break down margin by product line
