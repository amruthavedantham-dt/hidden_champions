Part 1

A real market leader, recovering from a rocky FY2024

This is a genuinely large, real business with a volatile recent history: revenue grew from ₹2,913 Cr (FY21) to ₹8,640 Cr (FY26), but FY2024 was a net-loss year (₹839 Cr loss) sandwiched between profitable years (₹105 Cr FY23, ₹155 Cr FY25, ₹228 Cr FY26). Current ROE (15%, both 1-yr and 5-yr average) and ROCE (14%) are genuinely healthy, though debt-to-equity of ~0.96 reflects meaningful leverage (₹1,426 Cr borrowings). Q4 FY26 showed strong momentum: revenue up 45.4% YoY.

It is confirmed, not just self-claimed, as India's largest non-ferrous metal recycler by installed capacity — independently corroborated at ~4x the nearest domestic competitor's capacity — with combined production capacity exceeding 615,000 MTPA across 13 recycling facilities, ~70% market share in India's liquid secondary aluminium market, and 30-35% share in the automotive secondary aluminium segment specifically. It sources scrap through a global network spanning 73 countries.

It holds real, verifiable joint ventures with major Japanese industrial partners: CMR Toyotsu (70:30 JV with Toyota Tsusho, established April 2014, two plants totaling 66,000 MT capacity), plus JVs with Nikkei MC Aluminium and Nippon Light Metal — genuine technology and capital partnerships, not marketing claims. Its DSIR-recognized in-house R&D unit is also independently confirmed, along with ISO 14001:2015, ISO 45001:2018, and IATF 16949:2016 certification.

Its 'Direct Liquid Metal Supply' and regenerative-burner technology are confirmed real, differentiated capabilities — eliminating a die-caster's own ingot-remelting step and cutting fuel/melt-loss waste, using regenerative burners, metal circulation furnaces, heavy media flotation, and induction/XRT/LIBS sorting across its plants. The June 2026 IPO was structured entirely as an offer-for-sale, meaning it raised no fresh capital for the company itself — a detail worth noting when assessing growth-funding capacity going forward.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A genuinely large, fast-growing India recycling market, with real policy tailwinds beyond generic ESG framing.

    India aluminium scrap market: $3.8B (2024) to $11.2B by 2034 (10.8%/yr); the Steel Scrap Recycling Policy 2019 and Vehicle Scrappage Policy both structurally increase non-ferrous scrap supply, directly feeding CMR's core input.

  • Two real, comparably-scaled listed peers exist, both showing strong recent growth — a genuine competitive benchmark, unlike most companies in this batch.

    Gravita India posted 9M FY26 revenue of ₹3,093 Cr (+9%) with PAT ₹286 Cr (+31%) and a ₹1,500 Cr capex plan targeting 700,000+ MTPA; Pondy Oxides & Chemicals posted Q1 FY26 revenue of ₹604.22 Cr (+35.56% YoY) with PAT up 94.21%.

  • Secondary aluminium's ~95% energy-cost advantage over primary production is a real, structural, permanent tailwind for the whole segment.

    This energy-cost gap, combined with automotive lightweighting/EV adoption structurally increasing demand for secondary aluminium alloys, is a durable driver independent of any single company's execution.

A genuinely large, verified market leader with real JV backing, real technology differentiation, and a fresh public listing at a strong premium — this is one of the most substantively documented companies in this batch. The FY2024 net-loss year is worth understanding (given it's recent enough to matter), but the overall trajectory into FY26 shows real recovery and momentum.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Product-Expansion

Expanding Direct Liquid Metal Supply and regenerative burner technology to more operations/customers

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly — broader application of these confirmed, real energy-saving technologies across the customer base — a credible lever given both technologies are already operational and independently verified, not aspirational.

Possibilities, not prescriptions — and the honest first step here is understanding what drove the FY2024 net loss.

Diagnosis

Understand the FY2024 net-loss driver before treating the recovery as fully secured

With FY2024 showing an ₹839 Cr net loss sandwiched between profitable years, confirming what specifically drove that loss (commodity price swings, one-off impairment, working-capital issue) would clarify whether the FY25-26 recovery reflects a resolved issue or ongoing volatility risk — particularly relevant now that the company carries public shareholder scrutiny post-listing.
