Part 1

A wealth-creation engine, with a pipeline that keeps it going

Revenue grew 23% to ₹299 Cr and PAT 52% to ₹38 Cr, with OPM at a healthy 22% and ROE at 22.3% — well above an industry average of roughly 8.9% cited in the same analysis, and ROCE at an even stronger 25.5%.

It holds IATF 16949 certification (since 2018) governing its full manufacturing process, and — per a third-party source, not just its own site — has achieved 'Self certification / Single source / Ship to use' status with leading auto brands, an unusually high trust tier that lets a supplier's parts go directly onto the OEM line without incoming inspection.

It has a robust pipeline of 51 new parts in development, targeting major customers including John Deere and Tata Motors — third-party confirmed, not a self-description — with over 50% of the new-business pipeline aimed at exports, where margins are better.

It commissioned a new Thrust Washer manufacturing unit (Unit III) with lead-free foundry technology in 2025, and won a $3.73M annual order from Allison Transmission plus a separate order for ~7.5 million pieces/year — both dated, specific wins, not vague growth claims. It operates across 24 countries, with export share currently ~30-34%, targeted to reach 37-40% by FY27.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and growing, though India-specific estimates spread widely.

    India bearings market: $6.71-9.18B by 2030-2032, 10.2-13.5%/yr depending on source; the narrower India automotive bearing sub-segment specifically is more mature, growing only 4.15%/yr.

  • EV adoption is a real long-term headwind for the core product, but near-term demand looks structurally sound.

    Pure engine bearings face zero demand from battery-electric vehicles, but final ICE-platform production waves and a large in-operation ICE parc sustain near-term demand — and Menon's own diversification into aluminum die-casting and braking systems hedges some of this.

  • The closest listed peer shows what real scale in this exact segment looks like, and where Menon still has room to grow.

    NRB Bearings posts ₹1,335 Cr revenue (~4.5x Menon's scale) with 77% PAT growth, and just approved ₹270 Cr combined capex to expand capacity 15-25% — a signal the whole segment is investing for growth right now.

A genuinely strong operator — healthy margin, high ROE, real single-source trust status with OEMs — with a specific, named pipeline of future business already in motion. This is one of the cleaner, stronger financial profiles across this whole batch.

Market data: Grand View Research, GlobeNewswire (shared engine-bearings-bushes-thrust-washers segment file). Peer data: NRB Bearings (much larger), Precision Camshafts (different product, weaker comparable).

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Velocity

Commissioning the dynamometer to enter the railway business

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: a dynamometer for the brakes division — needed to enter the railway business — has faced delays, holding back a genuinely new market entry that would sit alongside the company's existing bearings/bushes/aluminum-casting lines.

HVT gap research (finance.yahoo.com).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Menon Bearings' own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Set a firm commissioning date for the dynamometer and track it publicly

Given the railway-business entry has already been delayed once, set and communicate a specific commissioning timeline for the dynamometer, and track adherence to it as a leading indicator before assuming the railway opportunity is on track.
