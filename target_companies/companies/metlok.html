Part 1

A capability with a real technical edge — and an identity worth pinning down

Revenue sits in a ₹50-100 Cr band with a 25% one-year revenue CAGR; EBITDA and net profit both grew, though the two sources available (Tofler, Tracxn) give slightly different growth rates (25.9-28% EBITDA, ~39.5% net profit) and neither discloses the precise absolute figures behind them.

The technical differentiation is genuinely specific, not generic marketing copy: it developed in-house equipment and technology for microencapsulated adhesives in partnership with omniTECHNIK Microverkapselungs GmbH (Germany) and Nylok (USA) for fastener-coating adhesives — named partners, not a vague R&D claim.

It holds ISO 9001:2015 certification with an IATF-aligned management system, positioning it for automotive-grade qualification — the HVT sheet flags both `differentiation` and `moat` as unresolved despite `Verified` review status.

A genuine identity question sits underneath all of this: the HVT row's own sourcing (via metlok.in) describes an R&D/manufacturing facility in Kalmeshwar, Nagpur, and a network of sales offices and Precoat Service Centers across Bangalore, Chennai, Faridabad, Pune, Nasik, Mumbai and more — while separate research on the CIN in the HVT row (U24295KA2005PTC036555) traces to a Bengaluru-registered entity. Whether this is one integrated business with a Bengaluru registered office and a Nagpur plant, or two similarly-branded companies whose information has been conflated, was not resolved in this pass.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is large and steadily growing, led by global majors already investing heavily in India.

    Global adhesives and sealants: $77-86B (2025) to $115-130B by 2030-2034, 4.6-6.0%/yr. Henkel/Loctite alone has committed ₹400 Cr to an eighth India plant and completed a Phase III expansion near Pune.

  • India's own market is smaller but growing at a comparable pace.

    India adhesives: $3.28B (2026) to $4.58B by 2031, 6.88%/yr.

  • Automotive electronics is where the growth actually concentrates.

    ADAS sensors, camera systems and keyless-entry systems all require adhesives with electrical insulation, low ionic contamination, thermal stability and RoHS/REACH compliance — a more technical, higher-value niche than general-purpose bonding.

  • No clean public peer set exists at Metlok's scale — the named majors (Henkel, 3M, Pidilite) all operate at a materially different order of magnitude.

    Pidilite alone reports ₹14,600.83 Cr in FY26 consolidated net sales, though that spans far more than industrial adhesives specifically.

Real, named technology partnerships in a market where global majors are actively expanding India capacity. The company's growth rate looks credible; its corporate identity, on the evidence gathered so far, doesn't cleanly resolve to one clear picture yet.

Market data: Grand View Research, Mordor Intelligence. No clean size-comparable peer exists — named competitors are all multi-billion-dollar majors, included as market-structure reference points only.
