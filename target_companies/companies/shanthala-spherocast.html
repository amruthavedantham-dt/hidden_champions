Part 1

A real capacity bet, running into a real, dated sector shock

Revenue reached ₹148 Cr in FY25 (+9% 1-year CAGR, +16% EBITDA CAGR per Tracxn) — a genuinely growing foundry, though the specific PAT/EBITDA absolute figures found (₹10 Cr EBITDA, ~6.8% margin; ₹2.68 Cr PAT, ~1.8% margin) come from a search-engine summary of an Infomerics rating PDF that could not be independently re-verified this session (the document returned as image-based/non-extractable text) — treated as lower-confidence and flagged accordingly.

It manufactures grey iron, ductile iron, and alloy cast iron castings for automotive, industrial, energy, oil & gas, mining, hydraulics, construction, and pumps & valves sectors, holding ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015, ISO 45001:2018, and PED Certificate 2014/68/EU (pressure equipment) certification — a real, EU-regulatory-gated credential set that differentiates it from foundries unable to serve EU industrial-equipment OEMs.

A real, independently-confirmed capacity expansion is underway: a new Unit-2 with a high-pressure moulding line and continuous mixer, targeting 24,000 TPA (up from a current 10,000 TPA), backed by an envisaged ₹50 Cr investment — corroborated directly on the company's own IndiaMART listing, not just a self-claim from the HVT sheet.

The HVT sheet's own gap research names a real, currently-active headwind: production impact from West Asia conflict-driven raw material shortages and rising logistics costs. The exact 'Hindu/Shivamogga foundries' article and its specific '~20% production hit' figure could not be independently located this session, but the underlying macro claim is well corroborated by multiple, independently-dated 2026 trade-press sources — raw material costs surging up to 40% amid feared shortages, Strait of Hormuz disruption tightening industrial fuel and steelmaking input supplies, logistics cost escalation flagged by Indian exporters, and SMEs specifically named as most vulnerable given limited inventory-buffer capacity. Shivamogga's own foundry cluster (~45 foundries, ~5,500 employees, exports to 26 countries) sits squarely in this exposure zone.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • A large, genuinely fast-growing India casting market, with listed peers showing what a healthier margin profile looks like at scale.

    India Foundry and Casting Market: $22.0B (2025) to $57.9B by 2034 (10.78%/yr), India's fastest-growing APAC iron-casting sub-market; Kirloskar Ferrous (listed) posts ~11.7% 3-yr average ROE, and Nelcast (listed) posts an improving 9.3% PAT margin — both meaningfully stronger than Shanthala's low-confidence ~1.8% PAT margin estimate.

  • Larger listed peers increasingly integrate iron-making with casting to buffer exactly the kind of input-cost shock currently hitting the sector.

    Kirloskar Ferrous and Nelcast's forward integration reduces their exposure to the raw-material volatility that smaller, regional foundries like Shanthala are more exposed to — directly relevant given the current West Asia-conflict-driven shock.

  • PED 2014/68/EU certification is a real, specific EU market-access gate that Shanthala already holds — a genuine differentiator for pressure-equipment-linked customers.

    This certification specifically enables serving pumps/valves/oil&gas EU customers requiring pressure-equipment-rated castings, distinguishing certified suppliers from those who cannot access this regulatory-gated demand.

A real, mid-sized regional foundry making a genuine, capital-committed capacity bet (₹50 Cr for a 2.4x capacity expansion) at exactly the moment a real, dated, sector-wide raw-material shock is hitting Indian foundries. The timing tension is the honest story here: growth investment landing in the middle of a documented input-cost headwind, with thinner margins than the larger listed peers who've integrated further upstream.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities
