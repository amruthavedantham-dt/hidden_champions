Part 1

Profit compounding fast, on a still-thin margin

Revenue grew 32.65% to ~₹79.1 Cr and PAT grew 153.73% year-on-year — profit compounding far faster than revenue — but net margin remains razor-thin at 0.52% and ROE modest at 5.96% (ROCE just 3.13%), a company still converting scale into thin, if fast-improving, returns.

It manufactures plastic automotive parts for two-, three- and four-wheelers, processing 20 engineering plastic materials with an in-house paint shop — and, per its own account, launched an IoT-based real-time production tracking system with proprietary software, currently used internally for predictive maintenance and paperless factory operations.

A genuine identity caveat: the website the HVT sheet points to (sanjaygroup.in) actually describes a different, separately-incorporated group company — Sanjay Techno Plast Pvt Ltd (2004, TS16949-certified, 500+ employees across four plants). Those specific facts belong to that sister company, not to Sanjay Techno Products Pvt Ltd (this profile's subject, incorporated 1996).

It self-claims recent expansion into a brake-shoe assembly plant (2020) and an 'Ecosense' production plant (2023), plus a design/marketing office in Goa (2023), with two named subsidiaries (Ecosense Appliances Pvt Ltd, RytNow Systems Pvt Ltd) — none independently corroborated in this pass.

Part 2

The market it sells into

  • The field is growing steadily, though the HVT row's own cited market-size figure appears to contain a scale error.

    India automotive plastics: $1.51B (2025) to $2.22B by 2034 (4.25%/yr) per independent research — the HVT sheet's own alignment research cites a figure roughly 1,000x larger, almost certainly a units error in that source, not used here.

  • Lightweighting is a genuine structural tailwind, not just a talking point.

    Vehicle lightweighting trends are structurally increasing plastic content per vehicle across both ICE and EV platforms, and EV-driven component changes (battery enclosures, charging infrastructure) are creating entirely new plastic-part categories.

  • Listed peers in the broader auto-components space show the range of outcomes at scale.

    Craftsman Automation posts 21% revenue CAGR against an industry median of 11.89%, though its own ROE (8.65%, 3-yr avg 13.1%) shows even larger players don't automatically convert growth into high returns.

A fast-growing molder with a genuine internal tech asset (the IoT tracking system) sitting on margins that haven't yet caught up to the growth rate — and a website mix-up that makes its real scale harder to pin down than it should be.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

One clear place worth watching, named directly in the company's own gap research.

Margin

Commercializing the IoT production-tracking system as a service

The HVT sheet's own gap research names this directly: the proprietary IoT-based real-time production tracking system, currently used only internally for predictive maintenance and paperless operations, has explicit potential to be offered as a service to other manufacturers — a software-margin opportunity distinct from the company's thin-margin core molding business.

HVT gap research (LinkedIn/TheMachineMaker coverage).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Sanjay Techno Products' own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Margin

Pilot the IoT system with one external manufacturer

Before building a full commercial product, offer the existing IoT production-tracking system to one other small manufacturer (even a non-competing one) as a paid pilot, to test real-world demand and pricing before investing in productizing it further.
