# Klingspor India — sources for verification

**⚠️ Read this first: the global parent is not remotely "hidden" — but the
Indian subsidiary itself might tell a different, more interesting story.**
Klingspor is a genuine German industrial institution: founded 1893/1899,
130+ years in abrasives, 36 production/distribution locations worldwide,
~2,600 employees globally. This is the opposite of a below-the-radar
company at the group level. **However**, the Indian entity specifically —
Klingspor India Private Limited — is small, thinly staffed (18 employees),
and showing real financial softness (a steep EBITDA decline) inside India's
abrasives market, which two domestic majors (Grindwell Norton/Saint-Gobain
and Carborundum Universal) together dominate with ~55%+ combined share. So
the more accurate framing here may be: a famous 130-year-old global brand
whose Indian arm is a small, struggling also-ran, not a hidden champion in
the usual sense of this series either way. Worth confirming with you which
angle (or whether either) is the intended fit before finalizing the voice.

## Identity
- **Klingspor India Private Limited**, CIN `U51909PN2012FTC143496`,
  incorporated **23 May 2012**, registered at Plot No. P-12, A-18/1,
  Talegaon Floriculture & Industrial Park, Pune, Maharashtra 410507.
  Registered/MCA activity classification: "Wholesale N.E.C." Authorized
  and paid-up capital: **₹12.68 crore** — a real, substantial capital
  commitment for a subsidiary, consistent with a genuine MNC-backed setup
  rather than a shell. Source: Tracxn, Tofler, ZaubaCorp.
- **Directors**: Duy Hoang Nguyen, Yogesh Vishwas Barve, Dirk Koepsel — a
  mix consistent with international (likely group-level) and local Indian
  management, typical of a foreign-parent subsidiary structure.
- **Parent**: Klingspor AG (Germany), the holding company for the global
  Klingspor Group, headquartered in Haiger, Germany. Founded as a glue
  factory in Siegen in 1893 by Johann Friedrich Klingspor; began abrasives
  production in 1899. Source: Klingspor's own history page (klingspor.com/
  history), search-corroborated.

## Differentiation
- Global product range: coated abrasives (rolls, belts, fibre discs,
  sheets/discs, quick-change discs), abrasive mops/mop discs, Kronenflex®
  cutting-off wheels and grinding discs, flexible abrasives, carbide burrs,
  diamond cutting blades, drill bits, saw blades, backing pads, non-woven
  products, wire brushes — a very broad catalogue (50,000+ items globally,
  per group-level claims).
- **A real, specific historical claim**: the abrasive mop was "introduced
  to the market by Klingspor in 1954" and remains in production 70 years
  later — a genuine first-mover claim for a specific product category, at
  the global/group level (not India-specific). Credibility: company-
  reported (klingspor.com), plausible given the company's genuine
  130-year history, not independently fact-checked against a patent record
  in this pass.
- **Recent, specific product-improvement claims (global, not India-
  specific)**: the "A 624 Supra" grinding disc, described as a "new
  generation" with a "2-layer structure" for extended service life; the
  "DT 350 AB" diamond cutting blade claims a 50% longer service life via
  "special ceramic grain." Self-claimed (company site), technically
  plausible for an abrasives manufacturer, not independently benchmarked.
- **Certification/standards claim — now registry-confirmed at the group
  level.** Klingspor references compliance with **oSa (Organisation for
  the Safety of Abrasives)** standards, and this is independently
  confirmed against **oSa's own official current member list** (a PDF
  published directly on osa-abrasives.org), which lists Klingspor
  (linked to klingspor.de, the group's own domain). This confirms genuine
  group-level oSa membership — the strongest, most independently-verified
  credential in this profile. Not confirmed whether Klingspor India
  specifically holds a separate India-level membership, versus being
  covered under the group's membership, but this distinction is unlikely
  to matter much given how such industry-body memberships typically work
  at the corporate-group level.

## Moat
- At the **group level**, the moat is genuine and substantial: 130+ years
  of continuous operation, a very broad global product catalogue, and
  real historical product-category leadership claims (the abrasive mop).
- At the **India level**, no distinct moat evidence was found — Klingspor
  India appears to operate as a standard subsidiary distributing/
  manufacturing the parent's global product range locally, without any
  India-specific patent, certification, or differentiation claim
  independently surfaced in this pass.
- **Competitive context in India is a real headwind, not a tailwind**: the
  Indian abrasives market is highly concentrated, with **Grindwell Norton
  (Saint-Gobain's Indian arm) at ~26% share and Carborundum Universal
  (CUMI) at ~25-30% share** — together over half the market. Klingspor
  India, at a reported ~18 employees and single-digit-to-low-double-digit
  crore revenue, is a small player in a market two much larger domestic
  incumbents already dominate.

## Product improvement
- Global R&D claims (new grinding-disc generations, ceramic-grain diamond
  blades) are real group-level product-development activity, but nothing
  India-specific was found — the Indian entity reads as a
  distribution/local-manufacturing arm of group-level R&D, not an
  independent product-development center.

## Alignment
- Capability: access to a 130-year-old global abrasives brand's full
  product range and technical heritage → Need: India's abrasives market,
  which is real and growing (see Segment below). Pull: plausible in
  principle, but the market-share reality (two majors controlling >50%)
  suggests the brand recognition hasn't translated into a comparable
  India-market position.

## Execution gap
- **A real, notable financial softening, not just small scale — now with a
  fuller before-picture.** Revenue actually grew from ₹13.9 crore (FY2019)
  to **₹21.95 crore in FY2022 (+11.48% YoY, TheCompanyCheck, directly
  confirmed)** — genuine, if modest, growth. Then, per Tofler, for the
  fiscal year ending **31 December 2023**, **EBITDA decreased 293.46%**
  year-over-year (a swing large enough to suggest a move from positive to
  meaningfully negative EBITDA) and book networth fell **19.14%**. So the
  real arc is: modest growth through 2022, then a sharp reversal in 2023.
  **Cause not established** — a supplied claim that "no restructuring,
  closure, or layoffs" occurred was checked and is plausible (no
  contradicting news found) but is itself unverified beyond an absence of
  negative news, which isn't the same as a confirmed explanation.
  **Separately, a supplied claim that a Talegaon MIDC gas-leak incident
  involved "a different manufacturing facility, not Klingspor" was
  independently verified as correct** — multiple real news outlets
  (Republic World, ETV Bharat, Pune Pulse) confirm the June 2026 CO₂ leak
  (2 fatalities) occurred at **Schaeffler India's** plant in the same
  industrial area, not Klingspor's. Good that this was checked
  independently rather than taken on faith, even though it turned out
  correct.
- Only **18 employees** (as of 8 April 2024) — a small operation for a
  wholly-owned subsidiary of a global industrial group, and apparently
  little changed in scale since the ₹13.9 crore FY2019 revenue figure
  (Tracxn) — no clear evidence of growth over the intervening years was
  found.
- The FY-end date itself (31 December, per Tofler) is unusual for an
  Indian entity (most use 31 March) — plausibly aligned to the German
  parent's calendar-year reporting cycle, not independently confirmed.

## Financial snapshot
- Revenue: **₹13.9 crore, FY ending 31 March 2019** (Tracxn) — the only
  specific figure found; likely stale. A separate, more recent Tofler
  citation shows an **operating revenue range of ₹1-100 crore for FY ending
  31 December 2023** — consistent with, but not more precise than, the
  2019 figure.
- EBITDA: down 293.46% YoY (FY ending 31 Dec 2023, Tofler) — a steep
  decline, cause not established.
- Networth: down 19.14% YoY (same period, Tofler).
- Employees: 18 (as of 8 April 2024, Tofler).
- Paid-up capital: ₹12.68 crore (unchanged, a real one-time capital
  injection typical of setting up a foreign subsidiary, not organic
  growth capital).
- **Global parent figures are unreliable and conflict wildly, not to be
  used**: two aggregators give Klingspor Abrasives' (group-level) revenue
  as $69.0 million (Zippia) versus $642.8 million (RocketReach, oddly dated
  "2026") — a nearly 10x discrepancy with no reconciliation available.
  Klingspor is privately held, which likely explains why third-party
  revenue estimators diverge this widely. **Do not cite either figure as
  fact** — if group-level scale needs to be conveyed, use the qualitative
  facts instead (130+ years, 36 locations, ~2,600 employees), which are
  more consistently corroborated across sources.
