# SWAYAM (Swayam India) — sources for verification

## Identity — a real name-collision risk, resolved
"Swayam" is a very common Indian company name (Swayam Group/IT services,
Swayam healthcare kiosks — acquired by Mylab 2021, Swayam Cottons, Swayam
Automation, Swayam Analytics, Swayam Apparels, Swayam Creations all exist as
unrelated entities). The home-furnishing brand at swayamindia.com is **none
of these** — resolved via CIN and cross-confirmed by Tracxn directly:

- **The correct legal entity: Linen Design Company Private Limited**, CIN
  `U51909HR2008PTC037949`, incorporated **23 May 2008**, registered address
  Flat 701, Tower-II, Vipul Greens, Sector-48, Sohna Road, Gurgaon, Haryana
  122018. Paid-up capital ₹5 crore. MCA activity classification: "Wholesale
  N.E.C." Directors: **Manoj Garg and Neerja Garg**. **Tracxn explicitly
  states: "Linen Design Company Private Limited is the legal entity of
  Swayam India."** Source: [Zauba Corp](https://www.zaubacorp.com/LINEN-DESIGN-COMPANY-PRIVATE-LIMITED-U51909HR2008PTC037949), [Tofler](https://www.tofler.in/linen-design-company-private-limited/company/U51909HR2008PTC037949), Tracxn.
- **A second, related-but-distinct entity exists: Swayam India Private
  Limited**, CIN `U74999HR2017PTC069482`, incorporated 13 June 2017,
  registered address H.No-142, Munjal Colony, Gurugram 122003 — **same two
  directors/shareholders (Manoj Garg, Neerja Garg)**, but MCA classifies its
  activity as "Business Services," not textiles/wholesale. Likely a newer
  group vehicle (retail/e-commerce/holding arm launched alongside or after
  the website), but its exact function relative to Linen Design Company is
  **not resolved** — flagged so the two aren't conflated. The site's own
  "About Us" page states the brand is "owned by Linen Design Company Private
  Limited," which is consistent with treating Linen Design Company as the
  primary entity for financials/identity purposes here.
- **Founders: Manoj Garg and Neerja Garg, founded 2001** — confirmed by
  Tracxn directly and consistent with both entities' MCA director listings.
  Neerja Garg is separately described in press as holding a Master's in
  Textile and Clothing from Lady Irwin College, New Delhi (1987-1992) — a
  real, specific, plausible credential for a home-furnishings founder.
  **A conflicting claim found in lower-quality search results — that the
  founders were "Kushagr Dixit and Mr. Tanmay" — does not match any MCA
  director record for either entity and is rejected as unreliable**; a
  separate "founded 1999" date also appeared but is likewise not
  corroborated against the more authoritative Tracxn/MCA-consistent 2001
  date. Source: Tracxn, MCA director records (Zauba Corp/Tofler).
- Employees: 501-1,000 (Tracxn, as of ~July 2025).
- Gurugram-based; classified by Tracxn as "unfunded" (no external/VC
  capital — a bootstrapped, family-controlled business since 2001).

## Differentiation
- Products: bed sheets, curtains, cushion covers, table linen, kitchen
  linen, bags, diwan sets, kids' products, mattress protectors — a broad
  home-furnishings catalogue. Company site states: "All Swayam products are
  conceptualized, designed, and manufactured in our state-of-the-art
  facilities in Gurugram, India," emphasizing "Indian artisanship and
  sustainable, homegrown manufacturing." Credibility: self-claimed (company
  site). needs_verify: yes for the "state-of-the-art" characterization
  specifically; the in-house manufacturing claim itself is plausible given
  the 2008-incorporated wholesale/manufacturing entity backing it.
- **Retail scale claim**: "over 400+ counters in over 70+ cities across
  India" (company's own About Us page, directly consistent across
  press/directory sources — ExportersIndia, Global Trade Plaza). A separate
  franchise-partner-facing page reportedly shows "400+ Counters, 40+
  Cities" instead — not independently re-fetched in this pass to confirm,
  but plausible as a narrower "franchise/high-tier partner outlet" subset
  of the broader 70+-city distribution footprint rather than a
  contradiction. **New, more concrete corroboration found**: multiple
  independent search results name specific retail chains carrying Swayam
  products — **Home Stop, Shopper's Stop, Pantaloons, @Home, Hometown,
  EVOK, Spencer's, and Hypercity** — all real, well-known Indian retail
  chains, which is a more checkable, specific form of corroboration than
  the bare "400+ counters" figure alone. needs_verify: yes for the exact
  counter/city counts, though the named-retailer detail is a real step up
  in specificity.
  **Third figure now on record (2026-07-30)**: the company's own profile
  PDF states "200+ counters · 40+ cities" — the city count (40+) matches
  the franchise-page figure already on file, but the counter count (200+)
  is roughly half the 400+ figure used elsewhere, including on the
  company's own About Us page. Three different counter/city combinations
  now exist across company-controlled sources alone (400+/70+, 400+/40+,
  200+/40+) — not reconciled here; reported as-is rather than picking one.
  The PDF's offline-stockist list also differs slightly from the prior one:
  it names Shoppers Stop, @Home, Lulu, and Pai International, omitting

- **No patents, DSIR recognition, or specific proprietary-technology claims
  found** — expected for a textile/home-furnishings manufacturer; this is
  `unresolved`, not `commodity` (no positive finding either way).
- **No OEKO-TEX, GOTS, or ISO certification held** — directly stated by the
  analyst's own research pass: Swayam relies instead on domestic quality
  benchmarks (colorfastness, skin-safety/allergen testing) rather than
  international organic/ecological textile audits. Consistent with a
  domestic-retail-facing brand rather than an internationally-certified
  exporter.
- **DRDO/SITRA claim, corrected on a second pass: real, but narrower than
  first stated.** A follow-up, independently-sourced check (Business
  Standard, plus Swayam's own dated 30 April 2020 blog post and live
  Flipkart/Seniority.in product listings) confirms: during COVID-19, Swayam
  became a licensed PPE manufacturer (one of 500+ government-approved
  suppliers), producing 3-ply masks, coveralls, gloves, face shields, and
  shoe covers. The Ministry of Textiles specifically authorized **SITRA,
  DRDO, and the Ordnance Factory Board jointly** to issue "Unique
  Certification Codes" for PPE during this period — so the DRDO tie is real,
  but specifically to **2020-era pandemic PPE/mask certification**, not an
  ongoing "custom fabric engineering and medical textile lines" business
  division as first (and less accurately) framed. Business Standard
  separately describes Swayam as a "Rs 100-crore turnover" company in this
  2020 coverage — a different figure than Tracxn's ₹55.6cr FY25, plausibly
  reflecting either a different (possibly group-wide or COVID-boosted)
  period, imprecise press reporting, or both; not reconciled here.
  Source: [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/textile-firms-condom-makers-join-covid-19-fight-become-ppe-suppliers-120052500504_1.html), [Swayam's own blog](https://www.swayamindia.com/blog/index.php/2020/04/30/life-saving-ppe-kit-for-frontline-healthcare-workers/).

## Moat
- No registry-confirmed qualification (patent, DSIR, sector certification)
  found. The closest thing to a moat is (a) 24 years of continuous operating
  history under the same founder-family ownership (2001-present, real and
  MCA-consistent) and (b) real retail distribution scale (400+ counters/70+
  cities claim), though the latter is not independently itemized.
- **Competitive context**: the Indian home-textile market includes several
  much larger, listed players — Welspun India, Indo Count Industries,
  Trident, Bombay Dyeing & Manufacturing, Himatsingka Seide, GHCL, Raymond
  Home, Arvind (Home Textiles Division) — alongside branded peers closer to
  Swayam's own retail-facing positioning (Portico New York, Spaces, D'Decor,
  Sarita Handa, Fabindia, Maspar). Swayam, at ₹55.6 crore revenue, is a
  small-to-mid player relative to the large listed manufacturers, though
  scale-comparable to some of the branded retail peers (not independently
  sized against each by name in this pass).

## Segment
India home-textile / home-furnishing retail (bedding, curtains, table/
kitchen linen). Segment-tier research not yet built as a `segments/*.json`
file. Market sizing found but **carries a real internal inconsistency worth
flagging rather than smoothing over**: one set of sources (Custom Market
Insights) puts the whole "India Home Textile Market" at $11,723.6 million
(2024) while a separately-cited "India Bedsheet Market" is put at $19,717.6
million (2024) — a sub-category apparently larger than the whole category
it should sit inside. This is almost certainly a scope/definition mismatch
across two different research houses' methodologies (e.g., different
category boundaries or one figure including exports/institutional sales the
other excludes), not a real market fact — **do not use both figures
together without resolving the inconsistency**, and note it explicitly if
either is used alone. Separately, Facts & Factors puts India's home-textile
market at $10.78B (2023) heading to $23.32B (2032), 8.4-8.6% CAGR range
across sources — broadly consistent in growth rate even where absolute
size estimates diverge. Key named players: Welspun India, Indo Count
Industries, Trident, Bombay Dyeing & Manufacturing, Himatsingka Seide,
GHCL, Raymond Home, Arvind (Home Textiles Division), Portico New York,
Spaces, Sarita Handa, Fabindia, Maspar — a mix of large listed
manufacturers and branded retail peers.

## Growth trajectory (new from company PDF, 2026-07-30)
Company has publicly stated an ambition to reach **₹200 crore turnover**
with flagship stores in every Indian metro **by 2025**, plus a plan to
double retail outlets (multi-brand outlets and company showrooms). Three
named growth levers, per the company's own account (Industry Outlook,
2022): (1) opening owned PAN-India stores rather than relying mainly on
shop-in-shop shelf space; (2) deepening e-commerce listing quality across
the existing 5,000+ SKUs rather than adding more; (3) expanding
institutional/corporate-gift selling — a channel said to have already
built trust with 100+ corporate clients and cushioned pandemic-era impact.
Separately (The Franchising World, 2023), the company points to Tier 2/3
cities as the biggest under-penetrated opportunity for furnishing brands
generally, citing a 12–15 month ROI for franchise partners, and notes
e-commerce already reaches Tier 3 markets on its own. All self-claimed;
none of the targets, dates, or franchise-partner economics were
independently verified.
