## Moat
- **Registry-confirmed, mixed**: ISO 14001:2015 and ISO 45001:2018 are
  currently valid (expire March 2027); ISO 9001:2015 has expired (2024).
  A real but incomplete certification moat.
- **The real, load-bearing moat candidate is 67 years of continuous
  operation under the same founder-family (Soni family) ownership**,
  combined with a genuinely fast, real, recent growth trajectory (see
  Financial snapshot) — a rare combination of longevity and current
  acceleration, worth treating as the strongest signal in this profile.
- **A second, genuinely strong moat candidate, now confirmed**: founder
  **Mr. Sushil Chand Soni**, now 91 years old, is still personally active
  on the factory floor "almost every day," per the company's own LinkedIn
  post. He is credited with personally designing the company's coil-
  winding and forming machines — i.e., the company's own core production
  equipment was engineered in-house by its founder, not bought off the
  shelf. This is a specific, hard-to-fabricate, tacit-knowledge-based
  claim — the kind of institutional knowledge moat that doesn't show up
  in any registry but is genuinely difficult for a competitor to replicate.
  Source: [Dynamic Engineers' own LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dynamicengineerspvtltd_dynamicengineers-founderstory-legacyinmanufacturing-activity-7465655230756466688-VAvu/).
- Competitive context: India's organized electric-fan market is dominated
  by large, listed/branded names — Crompton Greaves, Havells, Orient
  Electric, Usha International, Bajaj Electricals, Polycab, Khaitan
  Electricals, Surya Roshni, Almonard, Luminous. Dynamic Engineers, at
  ₹100-500 crore revenue (FY25 band, Tracxn), is meaningfully smaller than
  these listed majors, consistent with a specialized/component-supply
  positioning rather than a head-on branded competitor.

## Execution gap
- **This is a growth story, not a decline story — unusual in this batch,
  worth leading with.** Two independent aggregators, covering two
  different (adjacent) periods, both show fast, real growth:
  - Tofler (as of FY ending March 2024): revenue ₹50-75 crore band, up
    **48.18%** YoY; EBITDA up 26.28%; Net Profit up 38.81%; Networth up
    12.01%.
  - Tracxn (FY ending March 2025): revenue ₹100-500 crore band, a
    **94%** one-year revenue CAGR, EBITDA CAGR 54%.
  Two consecutive years of strong, real growth for a 67-year-old company
  is a genuinely distinctive pattern — worth asking directly what's
  driving it (a new large client win, capacity expansion, category
  tailwind) rather than assuming it's simply organic drift.
- **A striking mismatch worth flagging, not smoothing over**: paid-up
  capital has stayed at just ₹10 lakh despite revenue in the ₹100-500
  crore band — this is unusual, though not necessarily a red flag for an
  old, privately-held, presumably-profitable family business that has
  simply never needed to raise capital via share issuance. Still worth
  asking about directly.

(2024)**, projected to reach **$3.72 billion by 2030** at an **11% CAGR**
