Part 1

A real founding story and real garden relationships, ahead of a settled financial record

Namhah was founded around 2015-2016 in Guwahati by Ankit Baid (CEO), whose family has reportedly been in the Assam tea business for over six decades, and Abhishek Chowdhury, who reportedly identified that premium Indian-grown tea was being sold back to Indian consumers at a markup via European luxury brands. The business is registered as Namhah Marketing, a proprietorship (GST-registered 1 July 2017) — not an incorporated company, confirmed independently twice; there is no CIN because none exists for this legal structure.

The business sources directly from 16 tea gardens/estates across Assam, Darjeeling, and Arunachal Pradesh, hand-picked by pluckers described as having 15+ years of experience, across 50+ varieties. It sells both direct-to-consumer (own site, Amazon, Flipkart, Snapdeal) and B2B (cafes, hotels, gifting curators, private-label brands), from a flagship store on GS Road, Christian Basti, Guwahati, with a team of roughly 5 people.

Funding history is genuinely disputed and stays unresolved here. One source (Inc42) describes the company as fully bootstrapped. A separate account (from the founder's 2018 YourStory feature) describes an initial ₹10 lakh self-funded start through March 2018, followed by an undisclosed seed/private investment in April 2018 to fund metro expansion, naming the platform "Ah Ventures" — but this could not be independently re-confirmed by the analyst, and the two accounts flatly disagree on whether any outside capital was raised at all.
Also unconfirmed: a claimed Tea Board of India exporter/trader registration (plausible — a real requirement for tea exporters — but not independently found in a direct search); customer-facing stats of "50+ million cups served" and a "4.9 rating from 1,200+ reviews"; and the specific content of the Telegraph and Economic Times features, whose existence is search-corroborated but whose text wasn't directly read.

Part 2

A fast-tripling niche inside a much larger, slower category

  • India's specialty tea market has roughly tripled in five years.

    From ~₹300 crore to ~₹1,000 crore, driven by Gen Z and millennial demand for premium, wellness-oriented tea — a much faster growth rate than the broader premium tea market (~4.2% CAGR through 2035).

  • Online distribution is a real structural advantage for a small, single-city brand.

    Online tea sales grew 9% in 2024 — D2C brands can reach national scale without building physical distributor networks, which matters directly for a Guwahati-based operation this size.

  • But at least one much larger, better-funded peer tells a similar story.

    Vahdam India, a D2C specialty tea brand with the same single-origin positioning, raised ₹25 crore from SIDBI Venture Capital in March 2025 and is scaling carbon-neutral certified exports.

A fast-tripling niche within a much larger, slower category — where Namhah's founding insight (the arbitrage between cheap Indian-grown tea and expensive European-branded resale) is genuine, but it now competes against at least one much better-funded brand telling a similar single-origin story.

Market data: ExpertMarketResearch, Fortune Business Insights, IMARC Group, Agro & Food Processing, Ken Research.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Three places worth a closer look, each about turning an unsettled claim into a clean fact.

The flat contradiction between "bootstrapped" and "raised an undisclosed 2018 round" is a real, closeable credibility gap — a single, consistent public statement would remove it.
