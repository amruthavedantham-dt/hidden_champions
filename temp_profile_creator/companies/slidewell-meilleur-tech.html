Part 1

A margin story, ahead of a capability story

Revenue grew 16.2% year on year to a ₹150–200 crore band in FY24 — but profit grew far faster, up 91.7%, with EBITDA up 50.5%. Profit is compounding roughly six times faster than the top line: real evidence of margin expansion inside an existing revenue base, not just growth.

Return on equity reached 8.78% and net profit margin 3.48%, on a small, unlisted balance sheet (₹6.4 crore paid-up against ₹10 crore authorized capital) with clean leverage — debt-to-equity of 0.09.

In business since 1988 and incorporated as a private limited entity in 2011, the company fabricates out of its Nashik base — window assemblies, vacuum circuit breaker panels, and vehicle-accessory components — for named customers spanning automotive, power, and rail-adjacent OEMs: Mahindra & Mahindra, ABB, Siemens India, JCB India, Crompton Greaves, and T I Cycles of India.

Confirmed via IAF CertSearch: the company's ISO 9001:2015 certificate (QM 01 00793/01), covering both its Nashik plants, is currently EXPIRED (a renewal may exist under a different certificate number, not yet located). Still not confirmed: no patent or DSIR-recognized R&D unit was found, its claimed Power Grid Corporation of India (PGCIL) vendor approval has no independent confirmation and was not raised on the company's own call, and its IATF 16949 (automotive) certification status has not yet been checked.

Part 2

Two growing markets, one shared bar

  • The auto-component field it sits in is large and compounding fast.

    India's auto-component industry turned over ₹7.6 lakh crore (~$85.9B) in FY2025-26, up 12.7% year on year, and has more than doubled in five years (~10.7–17% CAGR depending on period/source).

  • The power-equipment side it also serves is smaller but policy-backed.

    India's switchgear market is estimated at $5.4–$13.8B depending on scope, growing ~6–9%/yr, inside a broader T&D equipment market headed toward $33.7B by 2030 — backed by ~₹9.1 lakh crore (~$109B) of planned transmission capex by FY32 and the government's RDSS grid-upgrade scheme.

  • Both OEM buyers are converging on the same bar: certification, not price.

    IATF 16949 is described as the minimum standard to supply major OEMs (Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, Hyundai, Mahindra), and evaluators increasingly weight process control — SPC, automated inspection, full traceability — over unit price. This is exactly the bar Slidewell's (unconfirmed-current) certifications would need to clear.

A diversified fabricator sitting across two growing, certification-gated markets — India's compounding auto-component base and a policy-funded T&D buildout — where the real open question is not demand, but whether its own qualifications are current enough to keep clearing the bar its customers are raising.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Three places worth a closer look, each built on something already visible in the numbers or the market.

Margin

Renew the lapsed ISO 9001 certificate

IAF CertSearch confirms the ISO 9001:2015 certificate covering both Nashik plants (QM 01 00793/01) is currently expired — a renewal may exist under a new number but hasn't been located yet. Closing this out is inexpensive next to the OEM-eligibility risk of an active customer audit finding it lapsed.

IAF CertSearch, certificate QM 01 00793/01 (confirmed expired, checked 2026-07-18).

Margin

Price the qualification, not just the part

Profit growth (91.7%) already outpaced revenue growth (16.2%) — real margin expansion is underway. The higher-certification power/T&D work plausibly carries better economics than commodity auto/rail fabrication; worth testing whether mix, not just cost control, is driving the recent margin gain.

Velocity

One blue-chip roster, two growing markets

The same fabricator already supplies an auto/rail OEM base (Mahindra & Mahindra, T I Cycles) and a power-equipment base (ABB, Siemens, Crompton Greaves) — a natural bridge to lean harder into whichever market's capex cycle is moving faster in a given year.
