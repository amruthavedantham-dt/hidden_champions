Part 1

A multi-year margin question, not yet a growth story

Nav Bharati Chemicals Private Limited was incorporated on 4 July 2014 in Hyderabad, though the underlying brand has manufactured bleaching earth and bentonite products since 1993. It is led by directors Alok Nandkishore Bansal and Chakravarthy Kalyan Marisetty, on ₹1.1 crore of fully-paid capital, with a team of 25–26 people.

Revenue for the year ending March 2025 was ₹31.88 crore, down 5% year on year — a continuation, at a slower pace, of the decline seen in the last fully-detailed earlier snapshot (FY22), when EBITDA fell 54.46% and net profit was already negative. EBITDA kept falling into FY25 too, down 13%. A headline "+621% profit CAGR" figure also appears in one source, but FY25's actual net profit number is locked/undisclosed and FY22's base was already a loss — a large percentage swing off a negative base is not, on its own, evidence of a turnaround, and is not presented as one here.

The company manufactures activated bleaching earth/Fuller's Earth, activated carbon, and bentonite powder/granules, serving edible and non-edible oil refining, agriculture (fertilizer/pesticide additives), aviation turbine fuel filtration, and pet-care applications — with export claims to Malaysia, Indonesia, Nigeria, Bangladesh and other markets. Its stated R&D wing works on new bentonite-based formulations, though no patent or DSIR recognition was found for it.

Not confirmed: an active ISO, REACH, or DSIR registration; and the current status of a claimed Kosher (KLBD) certification, whose registry page could not be opened on two attempts. A separate check of these was made using an unverifiable source with no certificate numbers or registry links — it was rejected rather than reflected here.

Part 2

A growing commodity market, run on cost, not certification

  • The activated bleaching earth market is large and steadily growing.

    Global market size estimates for 2024 range from ~$2.6B to ~$3.0B, growing at roughly 5.2-5.9% a year. Edible oil and fats applications account for over 76.9% of global revenue, and Asia-Pacific holds over 64.6% of the market.

  • India's own import pattern points to future domestic refining demand.

    India imported 16 million tonnes of edible oil in 2025, with 99.7% of palm-group volumes arriving in unrefined (crude) form — a large gap that, if domestic refining capacity grows to close it, pulls directly on this product category.

  • This is a cost game, not a certification game.

    Global majors (Clariant, Imerys, Oil-Dri, Musim Mas, Taiko) hold ~40-45% combined share; the rest is split among small-scale regional producers competing chiefly on cost-per-tonne and consistent grade quality — a different dynamic than the certification-gated segments researched elsewhere in this project.

A small player in a steadily growing, globally-dominated commodity-mineral market, where competing on cost — not certification — is the real game, and where its own numbers show it has been losing ground on exactly that question for several years running.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Three places worth a closer look, grounded in a real peer comparison and a real structural demand signal.

Margin

Benchmark against the market leader

Ashapura Perfoclay, a listed peer in the exact same product category, grew net profit ~65% year on year (₹29 Cr to ₹48 Cr) on roughly ten times Nav Bharati's revenue, with EBITDA up 40%. Nav Bharati's own EBITDA kept declining over a comparable period — worth understanding what's driving the gap.

Ashapura Perfoclay financials: Tofler, Tracxn. Nav Bharati financials: Tofler, TheCompanyCheck, Tracxn.

Velocity

Ride India's growing edible-oil refining gap

India imported 16 million tonnes of edible oil in 2025, mostly unrefined — a structural signal pointing toward more domestic refining capacity ahead, which is exactly what consumes this product.

Import/refining structure data: industry research.

Close out one open certification this quarter

Kosher, ISO, and REACH status are all currently unresolved for this company — not confirmed active, not confirmed absent. Settling even one turns an open question into a sellable fact.
