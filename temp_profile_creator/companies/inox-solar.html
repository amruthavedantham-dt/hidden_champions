Part 1

A confirmed government qualification and a real plant, inside a much larger parent story

Inox Solar Limited was incorporated on 13 November 2024, registered in Vadodara, Gujarat, on ₹56.25 crore authorized / ₹50.11 crore paid-up capital — a serious capital base for a 20-month-old entity, consistent with a large group deliberately standing up a new vehicle rather than an organic startup. It is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Inox Clean Energy Limited, part of INOXGFL Group, led by a six-person board including Whole-Time Director Devesh Sharma.

Confirmed at the primary source: Inox Solar's entry on MNRE's own official ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) list, covering 1,274 MW of approved annual production capacity — TOPCon modules, Ahmedabad facility. This is a genuine government-registry qualification, not a self-claim, and it gates eligibility for government-funded solar tenders.

Two independent outlets (SolarQuarter and Economic Times), beyond earlier press found, confirm Inox Solar began commercial production at its Bavla, Ahmedabad plant in September 2025 — the first 1.2 GW phase of a planned 3 GW facility. The company's own site claims a further 6 GW already scaled (3GW India, 3GW USA) and targets an additional 4.8 GW module + 2.4 GW cell capacity across Odisha, Gujarat, and Assam — these broader figures are self-claimed and not independently itemized here.

The entity's own standalone revenue was under ₹10 crore for the year ending March 2025 — coherent, not concerning: the plant's commercial production only began in September 2025, after this reporting period closed. Separately, its parent, Inox Clean Energy Limited (not this entity directly), raised ₹3,100 crore in equity in January 2026 (CalPERS, SUN Group Global, Authum Investments; ~₹50,000 Cr pre-money) and a further ₹700 crore from Adar Poonawalla Family Office at a ₹70,000 crore valuation, and has filed a confidential DRHP with SEBI for a ₹6,000-10,000 crore IPO expected in 2026 — targeting 10 GW IPP + 11 GW solar manufacturing capacity and a projected ₹30,000 crore consolidated annual revenue at scale by FY28.

Part 2

A fast-growing, policy-built market — already crowded, and carrying its own overcapacity risk

  • India's solar manufacturing market is among the fastest-growing in this entire series.

    India's solar panel market is projected to grow from 12.4 GW (2025) to 83.7 GW by 2034 — a ~23% CAGR. The PV module market specifically is estimated at $9.5B (2025), heading to $20.9B by 2032.

  • This growth is real and policy-built, via ALMM and the PLI scheme.

    ALMM-listed capacity grew from 2.3 GW (2014) to 100 GW (Aug 2025); operational capacity now stands at 120 GW for modules and 29.3 GW for cells (June 2025). The PLI scheme supports 48.3 GW of cell-manufacturing expansion, ~36.5% already built. From June 2026, module makers must source cells only from ALMM List-II manufacturers.

  • But it's already crowded, with a real overcapacity risk, not just upside.

    Named incumbents dwarf Inox Solar's current scale: Waaree Energies (₹26,537 Cr FY26 revenue, +84% YoY, PAT ₹3,884 Cr, ~26 GW capacity, India's largest), Premier Energies (₹7,824 Cr revenue, 2nd largest), Emmvee, Vikram Solar, Saatvik Green, and Adani Solar (targeting 10 GW integrated capacity by mid-2026). Wood Mackenzie separately flags total sector capacity set to exceed 125 GW against just 29 GW of demand-side inventory — a real glut risk for new entrants.

A market growing near 20%+ a year, built up by real policy support — but one already crowded with much larger, well-capitalized incumbents, and carrying its own overcapacity risk. Inox Solar's edge isn't being early; it's arriving with a parent that can fund the scale-up the market's fastest-growing players are already running at.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Three places worth a closer look, each built on the one real, confirmed qualification already in hand.

Velocity

Lean on cell self-sufficiency ahead of the June 2026 rule

From June 2026, ALMM module makers must source cells only from List-II manufacturers. Inox Solar's own targeted 2.4 GW cell capacity, if built out in time, would make it vertically self-sufficient right as this rule takes effect — a real structural edge over module-only competitors, if it lands on schedule.

ALMM June 2026 rule: MNRE/IEEFA. Cell-capacity target: company site.

Velocity

Convert ALMM qualification into named government-tender wins

The 1,274 MW ALMM listing is real and confirmed. The next proof point is winning actual government-tendered capacity allocations against much larger established players — a named, verifiable win would do more for credibility than the capacity-target claims alone.

MNRE ALMM PDF.

Margin

Don't scale into the overcapacity problem blindly

Wood Mackenzie flags a real sector-wide overcapacity risk (125 GW capacity against 29 GW inventory). Scaling from 1.2 GW toward the targeted 11 GW+ needs demand-matched pacing, not capacity built for its own sake.

Wood Mackenzie overcapacity research.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only the company's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Publish real, dated progress toward the targeted 2.4 GW cell capacity — the concrete milestone that unlocks the vertical-integration advantage ahead of June 2026.
