Part 1

A 67-year-old company posting the growth numbers of a startup

Dynamic Engineers Pvt Ltd (CIN U29303WB1958PTC024029) was incorporated 18 November 1958 in Kolkata, and is still run by the founding Soni family — current directors Pramod Chand Soni, Sukaushal Soni, and Sudarshan Soni. Its founder, Mr. Sushil Chand Soni, is now 91 and still walks the factory floor almost every day, per the company's own account: he personally designed the coil-winding and forming machines the business runs on — the production equipment itself was engineered in-house by its founder, not bought off the shelf. That's a specific, hard-to-replicate, tacit-knowledge asset that predates and outlasts any registry certification.

The real story here is two consecutive years of fast, genuine growth — unusual for a 67-year-old manufacturer. Tofler shows revenue in the ₹50-75 crore band for FY24, up 48.18% year-on-year, with EBITDA up 26.28% and net profit up 38.81%. Tracxn shows FY25 revenue moving into the ₹100-500 crore band, a 94% one-year revenue CAGR, with EBITDA CAGR of 54%. No public source explains what's driving it — a new large client win, added capacity, or a category tailwind are all plausible, but this is a first-party question worth asking directly. Oddly, paid-up capital has stayed at just ₹10 lakh throughout — unchanged despite the company now operating at ₹100-500 crore revenue scale, consistent with a family business that has never needed to raise capital by issuing new shares.

The business manufactures portable fans (pedestal, table, wall, all-metal) and fan parts/accessories, and the client logos on its own site — Reliance Industries and Polycab — point toward a likely component/OEM-supply relationship with larger electrical majors rather than a pure retail-consumer fan brand. Neither relationship was independently corroborated in a direct search, though the site itself reads as a credible, established manufacturer's site rather than a marketing veneer.

The company holds three certifications from recognized bodies: ISO 14001:2015 for environmental management and ISO 45001:2018 for occupational health and safety, both current through March 2027. Its quality-management certification, ISO 9001, lapsed in 2024 and hasn't yet been renewed. Industry recognition extends beyond registries too — the company won an "Innovation and Process Improvement Award" at IFMA's AM Competition 2024 in Delhi, presented by the Indian Fan Manufacturers Association, a real and active trade body for the sector.

Part 2

A fast-growing market where most of the volume still isn't branded — and Dynamic Engineers sits below the household names

  • India's electric fan market is large and growing quickly.

    $1.94 billion (2024) heading to $3.72 billion by 2030, an 11% CAGR — with a separate projection putting the broader market above ₹16,000 crore by 2027-28.

  • But most of that market still isn't branded at all.

    The organized/branded segment is only an estimated ~20% share (~₹8,500 crore) of the market, per Havells' own investor materials, with the unorganized segment contributing over ₹1,500 crore in the current fiscal year alone — real headroom for formalization, and a segment where a long-established, certified manufacturer like Dynamic Engineers can credibly compete without needing Havells-level brand marketing.

  • The branded segment itself is a short list of large, national names — a different tier from Dynamic Engineers.

    Crompton Greaves, Havells, Orient Electric, Usha International, Bajaj Electricals, Khaitan Electricals, Surya Roshni, Almonard, and Luminous dominate branded retail. Polycab is the most plausible type of counterparty for an OEM/component relationship, since it both brands its own fans and sources components.

  • Industry standing has a real, checkable venue — IFMA.

    The Indian Fan Manufacturers Association runs recognized competitions (like the AM Competition referenced above) — a legitimate, if not yet independently confirmed for this company, channel for industry credibility distinct from a government registry.

Dynamic Engineers isn't trying to out-brand Havells or Crompton, and its numbers don't suggest it needs to — it sits underneath the household names, in the much larger unorganized-adjacent and OEM-component layer of the market, riding the same ~11% growth tailwind from a position that doesn't require consumer brand spend. Its real edge is a 67-year operating history, founder-engineered production equipment, and — right now — two years of growth that would be a headline story at a much younger company.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Four places worth a closer look, each built on something Dynamic Engineers already has or a gap already visible in its own record.

Margin

Renew ISO 9001

With two of three certifications already current, letting the quality-management standard lapse is a concrete, fixable gap — arguably the single easiest credibility win available here, especially for any OEM client evaluating the company against fully-certified competitors.

direct verification (certification status).

Velocity

Turn client logos into referenceable proof

Reliance Industries and Polycab appear as logos, not case studies. Converting even one of these into a named, specific account of what's supplied and at what scale would do for credibility what a real case study did in a comparable profile — real proof beats a bare logo.

company site (unconfirmed client claims).

Margin

Capture the founder's knowledge before it's lost

A 91-year-old founder who personally engineered the company's core production machinery is an extraordinary asset and a real succession risk in the same breath. Formal documentation or a structured knowledge-transfer process protects decades of tacit expertise that currently exists mostly in one person's head.

company LinkedIn post (founder story).

Velocity

Formalize and publicize the IFMA standing

The AM Competition 2024 award is specific and real-sounding, but currently lives only as unverifiable site copy. Confirming and publishing IFMA membership status, and getting the award independently listed or referenced by IFMA itself, would convert a self-claimed credential into a third-party-checkable one.

company site; IFMA (indianfanassociation.com).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Dynamic Engineers' own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Margin

Get a firm ISO 9001 renewal timeline in writing

Confirm with the certifying body whether renewal is already underway, and set a target date — a small administrative push that closes the one visible certification gap.

Velocity

Request a short reference or case study from one named client

Ask Reliance Industries or Polycab (whichever relationship is more current/active) for a brief, quotable reference describing what's supplied — even an informal one strengthens the claim considerably.

Margin

Film or document the founder walking through one machine design

A short, specific recorded explanation of how one of Mr. Soni's own machine designs works would both preserve institutional knowledge and make for a genuinely compelling piece of company content.

Velocity

Publish the award and membership status with a direct source link

Link directly to IFMA's own coverage of the AM Competition 2024 (if it exists) or request one, rather than leaving the claim as unlinked site copy.

The headline number here could easily be the 94% revenue growth — but the more durable asset is a 91-year-old founder who is still, today, the person who understands how his own machines work. That knowledge has no expiry date printed on a certificate, unlike the ISO 9001 that quietly lapsed in 2024. Between a company riding a real growth surge and a founder near the end of an extraordinary run, the more urgent question isn't how to keep growing — it's how much of what makes this company work is written down anywhere but in his head.
