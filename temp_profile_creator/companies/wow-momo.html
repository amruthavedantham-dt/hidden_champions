Part 1

A QSR brand's second act: turning itself into a packaged-goods company

Revenue grew from ₹413 crore (FY23) to ₹470 crore (FY24, +14%) to ₹640 crore (FY25, +36%) — a real acceleration, not a slowdown. EBITDA swung from a ₹1.7 crore loss (FY23) to ₹38.2 crore positive (FY24), though net loss stayed flat at ₹114 crore both years — the operating business is working; something below the operating line (depreciation, interest, or non-cash ESOP-type costs typical of a scaling VC-backed chain) is the drag still to close before the targeted ₹1,000-1,200 crore, IPO-ready revenue by 2027.

Investors have kept re-upping across a decade: $179M+ raised over 16 rounds since 2015 (Tiger Global, Lighthouse, Khazanah Nasional Berhad), valuation climbing from ~₹300 crore to ₹1,225 crore. 2025 alone saw a three-round bridge — ₹150 crore from Khazanah, 360One, and, notably, Haldiram's Kamal Agarwal personally (a rival/adjacent snack-and-QSR promoter backing this one), then ₹75 crore in December from veteran investor Madhusudan Kela — followed in 2026 by ₹110 crore and then ₹185 crore in debt (InCred, RevX Capital, Anicut Capital) to fund the store build-out below. Repeated, overlapping institutional conviction across many rounds and years is itself a signal, even without a single knockout number.

The real story here is the FMCG pivot. In February 2023, Wow! Momo hired Mithun Appaiah — a 20-year FMCG veteran (Nestlé, United Spirits/UB Group, Britannia, ex-CEO of Innovative Foods Ltd/Sumeru) — as CEO of a dedicated FMCG vertical, run through Wow Momo Sales Private Limited. Three years on, that business has crossed ₹100 crore in Annualized Run Rate, growing ~100% year-on-year, distributed to 450+ cities and 6,000+ retail touchpoints, live on every major quick-commerce app (Blinkit, Instamart, Zepto, Flipkart Minutes, BigBasket, Amazon) and in 1,000+ Reliance Retail stores. Its Cuppa Noodles line alone runs at roughly ₹3 crore/month, and a Korean pouch-noodle range launched January 2026 as a new ambient-foods category entry — a restaurant chain building an actual packaged-goods business from a standing start, not just licensing its name onto a shelf product.

The core restaurant business remains large and still growing: 850+ company and franchise stores across 95+ cities (~200 added in the past year), 6,067 employees, and four sibling brands under one roof (Wow! China, Wow! Chicken, Wow! Kulfi, China Belly). The core "Wow Momo" mark has carried a filed trademark since 2010 — no patents or DSIR recognition, expected for a restaurant business; the moat, such as it is, is brand recognition plus funded distribution scale, not registry-based IP.

Part 2

A large, fast-growing, already-crowded domestic market

  • India's QSR market is large and growing near double digits.

    $27.80B (2025) → $30.37B (2026) → $47.28B by 2031, a 9.26% CAGR — driven by urbanization, rising disposable income, delivery-app growth, and Tier-2/3 expansion.

  • The organized/branded segment is taking share faster than the market itself.

    Branded chains are growing at roughly 15% CAGR (2023-2026) versus the broader market's ~9%, meaning chains like Wow! Momo are winning share from unorganized/local competition, not just riding overall demand growth.

  • Quick commerce has opened a real, structural distribution channel for QSR brands crossing into packaged retail.

    Blinkit, Instamart, Zepto, and Flipkart Minutes have become standard shelf space for frozen/ambient food brands — exactly the channel Wow! Momo's FMCG arm is already using at 6,000+ touchpoints.

  • Against the closest scaled peer, Wow! Momo is smaller but closer to operating profitability.

    Rebel Foods (EatSure), another VC-backed, IPO-track, multi-brand Indian food company, posted ₹1,617 crore revenue in FY25 (~3.4x Wow! Momo's ₹640cr) but was still EBITDA-negative at -8% margin (improving from -12%) with a ₹336.6 crore net loss. Wow! Momo, at a fraction of the scale, was already EBITDA-positive in FY24.

Worth being direct about: this is not an under-the-radar company in an obscure niche. It's a household QSR name in a large, fast-growing, but genuinely crowded market, with much bigger IPO-track peers chasing the same investor base. The distinctive thing isn't the restaurant business — it's the funded, fast-scaling FMCG business built inside it over the last three years, which is a much less obvious story than the brand's restaurant-chain fame suggests.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Four places worth a closer look, each built on something Wow! Momo already has running.

Margin

Convert the EBITDA swing into an actual profit

EBITDA moved from a ₹1.7 crore loss to ₹38.2 crore positive in one year, yet net loss stayed flat at ₹114 crore. Whatever sits below the operating line (financing costs, depreciation, non-cash charges) is now the single clearest lever left before the 2027 IPO target.

Entrackr; Inc42 (FY24 financials).

Velocity

Push the FMCG platform past its early beachhead

₹100 crore ARR at ~100% YoY growth in three years is a real result, but it's still a rounding error next to India's broader packaged-snack and instant-noodle categories. The distribution (quick commerce, Reliance Retail) is already built; the next test is whether growth holds as the base gets bigger.

Indian Retailer; Storyboard18; India Retailing.

Velocity

Execute the already-funded 1,500-store plan

The December 2025 and 2026 raises were explicitly earmarked to grow the network from ~800-850 to 1,500 stores within two years. This isn't a hoped-for expansion — it's funded and named; the opportunity is disciplined execution against a real number and timeline.

Agro & Food Processing (Kela raise coverage).

Margin

Cross-sell across the four-brand portfolio

Wow! China, Wow! Chicken, Wow! Kulfi, and China Belly all share the same distribution and operating backbone as the core momo brand. How much of the 850+ store footprint already carries more than one brand, and how much revenue that unlocks per outlet, isn't public — a natural next question.

company site; press (multi-brand confirmation).

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only Wow! Momo's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Margin

Trace the FY24 gap between EBITDA and net income line by line

Break the ₹114 crore net loss into its components (interest, depreciation, ESOP, one-offs) and identify which piece is structural versus which shrinks naturally as EBITDA keeps improving.

Velocity

Track FMCG cohort growth, not just the headline ARR

Segment the ₹100cr ARR by channel (quick commerce vs modern trade vs own site) and by product line (frozen momos vs Cuppa Noodles vs the new Korean noodles) to see which is actually compounding fastest before allocating further marketing spend.

Velocity

Publish a quarterly store-count milestone against the 1,500 target

A public, dated cadence toward the stated 2-year 1,500-store goal would let the market (and future investors) verify execution in real time rather than waiting for the next funding announcement.

Margin

Pilot co-located multi-brand outlets in a handful of cities

Test a small batch of stores carrying two or more of the four brands under one roof/kitchen and measure incremental revenue per square foot against single-brand outlets.

The famous part of Wow! Momo — 850+ stores, a household name, a decade of institutional funding — is not the interesting part. The interesting part is the three-year-old FMCG business running underneath it, already at a ₹100 crore pace and growing faster than the restaurant chain itself. For a company this well-known, the next chapter of value creation is happening in a place most people watching the brand aren't looking.
