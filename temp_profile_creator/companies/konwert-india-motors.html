Part 1

A real product and one confirmed pitch, ahead of the revenue and the wider claims

Konwert India Motors Private Limited was incorporated in Coimbatore in August 2019, founded by mechanical engineer Senthil Nickendra Manikandan. It builds retrofit kits that convert existing ICE auto-rickshaws to electric — the KIM JET 180 (₹2.25L, 180km range, 76.8V LFP battery, 42% gradeability, 60-minute fast charge) and the KIM DHOOR 120 (₹1.75L, 120km range, 72V NMC battery, 4.5-hour home charge) — with a claimed 99-minute ICE-to-electric installation process.

Revenue is genuinely small and falling: ₹23,600 in FY22, down to ₹4,664 in FY24 — a 98% year-on-year decline — confirmed directly against TheCompanyCheck's filed-financials record. This is a pre-revenue-stage company by most standards, not a company with an established sales base. The company is a DPIIT-recognised startup (a registration status, not a competitive credential). Separate Tofler figures (including a "+190.59% net profit" change) could not be independently verified — that page stayed paywalled on direct inspection — and are not treated as confirmed facts here.

Confirmed: Forge Innovation & Ventures' own account describes the 3 May 2024 "MSH Investor Connect" event at IIT Madras Research Park, where Konwert was one of 11 startups supported by MeitY under schemes including SAMRIDH and TIDE 2.0 that pitched to investors — with Keiretsu Forum among the investor organizations present. This confirms real accelerator-program participation and a real pitch in front of Keiretsu Forum; it does not confirm that Keiretsu Forum itself invested or backed the company.
Not confirmed: a specific CIIE.CO/IIMA Ventures "₹50 lakh shortlisting," an EESL "Facebook video" validation, and specific myTVS garage/phone-number integration all lacked any verifiable citation and were rejected as sources. ARAI certification for both kits is plausible — ARAI issues type-approval certificates directly to manufacturers rather than publishing a public registry — but remains unconfirmed independently. A claim of "500,000+ drivers empowered" and "10+ countries" of deployment sit in real tension with the company's four-figure annual revenue.

Part 2

A real, fast-growing market — but retrofit is a narrow, uncounted slice of it

  • India's electric three-wheeler market is large and growing fast.

    Estimated at $1.33-1.62B (2025-26), projected to reach $3.4-3.8B within a decade, growing at roughly 12-14% a year. Electric three-wheelers now make up 57% of all three-wheeler sales in India, with 699,073 units sold in FY2025 alone (+11% YoY).

  • But retrofit kits specifically are a narrow, largely uncounted slice of that.

    Most market-sizing research explicitly excludes kit retrofits from its scope, focusing instead on purpose-built electric vehicles — meaning the large headline figures above overstate the retrofit-specific opportunity Konwert actually competes in.

  • It's also already a populated field, not a blue ocean.

    Named competitors already active include RACEnergy (Hyderabad, ~₹50,000 battery-swap 3W kit, claimed 40-50% running-cost savings and a 12-15 month payback), GoGreenBOV, GOGOA1, and doReal Motors.

A real, fast-growing underlying market, but one where retrofit itself is a narrow, uncounted slice of it, competing against several other named kit-makers already at work — not a space Konwert has to itself.

Market data: IMARC Group, Mordor Intelligence, MarkNtel Advisors, ExpertMarketResearch. Competitor landscape: e-vehicleinfo.com, DIYguru, The Better India.

Part 3

Constraints, read as opportunities

Three places worth a closer look, each built on turning something real into something provable.

Velocity

Convert real accelerator exposure into a real customer base

The Forge/SAMRIDH pitch is genuine and dated — a real credential. The next step is turning that exposure into paying installs, since revenue hasn't yet caught up to the story it's built on.

Forge Innovation & Ventures LinkedIn post; financial snapshot: TheCompanyCheck, Tofler.

Margin

Match the claims to the numbers

Several public claims (500,000+ drivers, 10+ countries, specific myTVS integration) don't currently hold up to independent checking. Substantiating them with real, citable data — or scaling the public story back to what's provable — will matter more with the next investor or partner conversation than the claims themselves.

This research's own verification attempts, several rejected as unconfirmed.

Velocity

Compete explicitly on payback period

RACEnergy, a close peer, leads with a concrete, provable number: a 12-15 month payback and 40-50% running-cost savings. Konwert's own materials don't appear to lead with an equivalent, provable figure the same way.

RACEnergy figures: The Better India, industry coverage.

Possibilities, not prescriptions. Which would work, and how much, only the company's own data and people can tell. Each is small, made to learn fast.

Velocity

Publish real deployment numbers

Even a modest, real, verifiable install count would carry more weight with investors and partners than an unconfirmed 500,000-driver claim.

Margin

Get one institutional relationship on the record

Ask myTVS, EESL, or CIIE.CO directly for a citable, public confirmation — a joint press release or a portfolio-page listing.

Velocity

Publish a real payback-period calculator

Benchmark KIM JET 180 and KIM DHOOR 120's real operating economics against RACEnergy's clear 12-15-month payback claim.
